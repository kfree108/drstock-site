Amazon FBA Monthly Fee: What You're Actually Being Charged

Updated November 17, 2026 · By Ken Freeman · 1512 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon fba monthly fee”
The short answer

There's no single "FBA monthly fee." The $39.99/month charge is Amazon's Professional selling plan, not FBA. FBA itself charges a per-unit fulfillment fee plus a monthly storage fee per cubic foot, both variable, and storage, not the flat plan fee, is the recurring cost that adds up.

What people actually mean when they ask this

Search "Amazon FBA monthly fee" and you'll usually land on the $39.99/month number. According to Amazon's seller pricing page, that is the Professional selling plan, the account tier that lets you list in bulk, run ads, and use FBA in the first place. The alternative, the Individual plan, has no monthly charge at all; instead you pay $0.99 per item sold.

FBA is a separate layer on top of whichever plan you're on. It charges you to store and ship your inventory: a fulfillment fee per unit shipped, based on size and weight tier, and a storage fee billed monthly per cubic foot of space your stock occupies. Neither changes whether you're on Individual or Professional. Conflating the plan fee with FBA fees is the most common misunderstanding in this search.

The fees that recur every month, and what drives each one

Five charges show up on a seller's account with any regularity. Two come from your plan choice, three are driven by FBA and your inventory behavior (figures per Amazon's pricing page):

  • Selling plan fee: $39.99/month flat (Professional) or $0.99 per item (Individual). Fixed regardless of how FBA performs.
  • Referral fee: a percentage of each sale, set by category, with a minimum on most categories. Charged per sale, not monthly, but it's usually the biggest line item.
  • FBA fulfillment fee: per unit shipped, set by size tier and weight. Get the dimensional weight wrong and you're paying the wrong tier on every unit.
  • FBA storage fee: billed monthly per cubic foot of space used, with higher rates in the October to December peak window.
  • Aged inventory surcharge: added on top of storage once units sit in a fulfillment center past a set threshold, and it keeps building the longer that stock stays put.

A worked example: what a $50 item actually costs

For example, take a $50 item in Home and Kitchen with a 15% referral fee. That's a $7.50 referral fee per sale, and 15% of $50 clears the $0.30 minimum easily.

Now spread the plan fee. If you sell 40 units in a month, the $39.99 Professional fee is about $1.00 per unit; at 400 units it's about $0.10 per unit. The plan fee is the number most sellers fixate on, and at any real volume it matters least.

What doesn't shrink is the fulfillment fee (tied to the item's size and weight tier; use Amazon's own Revenue Calculator for your exact dimensions, since Amazon flags its figures as estimates) and the storage fee, which bills every month unsold stock sits in a fulfillment center.

To see your own number, write the month down in this order:

  • Plan fee: $39.99, or $0.99 times units sold on Individual.
  • Referral fees: sale price times your category percentage, times units.
  • Fulfillment fees: the per-unit fee for your size tier, times units.
  • Storage: cubic feet stored times the current monthly rate on your rate card.
  • Surcharges: any aged-inventory line on your fee report.

Across our measured portfolio at Full Circle (100+ brands managed, $500M+ in Amazon revenue managed), storage and aged-inventory charges are the fee lines that grow quietly while sellers watch the plan fee.

Individual or Professional: the break-even

The plan choice is the one fee decision that is pure arithmetic. Divide $39.99 by $0.99 and you get about 40.4, so the break-even is 41 items a month. For example, at 30 items Individual costs $29.70 against $39.99; at 100 items it costs $99.00 against $39.99.

Two caveats. First, Professional is the tier that unlocks bulk listing and advertising, so a seller who needs ads is usually on it regardless of volume. Second, check Amazon's current page before you switch, since plan terms can change.

The mistake sellers make chasing the wrong fee

The instinct when margin gets tight is to downgrade from Professional to Individual to "save" the $39.99. Under about 40 units a month that works. Above it, Individual costs more, and either way it does nothing to the fulfillment, storage or referral fees, which charge the same on both plans.

We've seen the inverse mistake just as often in client audits: a team spends an afternoon debating the $39.99 line while a single slow-moving SKU has been building storage and aged-inventory surcharges for months. The plan fee is visible and fixed, so it feels like the lever to pull. It rarely is.

When the fee number looks wrong

Sometimes the charge itself is the problem, not your understanding of it. Check these before you assume the fee is correct:

  • Fulfillment fee looks too high: check the size and weight tier Amazon has assigned the ASIN. Dimensional-weight misclassification is common and it's disputable.
  • Storage fee climbing month over month: usually a sign a SKU needs a removal-versus-liquidation decision before the aged-inventory surcharge stacks further.
  • A shipment came in short or damaged: that's a reimbursement claim, not a fee dispute, and it has its own filing window.

If the number you're questioning is a cost-per-click or an ACOS rather than a fee on your seller account, that's a different problem; send it to Dr. PPC. Everything above is the warehouse side of the ledger, not the ad account.

A monthly fee audit you can run in 20 minutes

Run this once a month, with your fee report open next to your inventory report:

  1. Confirm which selling plan you were billed on and whether your unit count still justifies it.
  2. Sort units by storage cost and flag any SKU that sells slowly relative to the stock held.
  3. List every unit near the aged-inventory threshold and decide: promote, remove or liquidate.
  4. Spot-check the size and weight tier on your five best sellers against the product's real dimensions.
  5. Compare units sent to Amazon with units received; any gap is a reimbursement claim.

Which one you should actually pick

Most sellers can work out the plan fee and referral fee with a calculator; that part is arithmetic, not strategy. Where it gets hard is catching the errors: a misclassified fulfillment tier, a SKU that should have been liquidated months ago, a reimbursement nobody filed. That's the layer Stock works, as an audit alongside FBA, not a replacement for it.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Total three numbers first: storage and aged-inventory surcharges for the last twelve months, lost sales on days your best sellers were out of stock, and cash sitting in SKUs that have not moved in 180 days. Then ask each vendor what they would do about those three in week one.

Common questions

Is the $39.99 fee the FBA fee?

No. The $39.99/month charge is Amazon's Professional selling plan, payment for the account tier, not for fulfillment. FBA fulfillment fees, storage fees and referral fees are separate charges that apply on the Individual or Professional plan.

Do I still pay FBA fees if I choose the Individual plan instead of Professional?

Yes. The Individual plan swaps the $39.99/month for $0.99 per item sold. That's a selling-plan choice, not an FBA choice. If you use FBA to store and ship, the fulfillment fee, storage fee and referral fee apply the same either way.

How much is the FBA storage fee per month?

It's billed per cubic foot of space your inventory occupies in the fulfillment center, and the rate changes with the calendar, rising in the October to December peak window. The current rate lives on your Seller Central rate card; check it there rather than trusting a number in any article, since it moves.

At what point does the Professional plan become cheaper than Individual?

Divide $39.99 by $0.99 and you get about 40.4, so from 41 items a month Professional is cheaper and below that Individual is. It's arithmetic on two published prices, so recheck them on Amazon's pricing page before deciding.

What's the biggest FBA monthly cost sellers miss?

Storage on slow-moving stock. The plan fee and referral fee are visible and predictable. Storage fees repeat every month on inventory that isn't selling, and the aged-inventory surcharge stacks on top once units sit too long.
