Amazon FBA Monthly Fee: What You're Actually Being Charged

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1321 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon fba monthly fee”
The short answer

There's no single "FBA monthly fee." The $39.99/month charge is Amazon's Professional selling plan, not FBA. FBA itself charges a per-unit fulfillment fee plus a monthly storage fee per cubic foot, both variable — and storage, not the flat plan fee, is the recurring cost that actually adds up.

What people actually mean when they ask this

Search "Amazon FBA monthly fee" and you'll usually land on the $39.99/month number. That's real, but it's not an FBA fee — it's Amazon's Professional selling plan, the account tier that lets you list in bulk, run ads, and use FBA in the first place. The alternative, the Individual plan, has no monthly charge at all — instead you pay $0.99 per item sold.

FBA is a separate layer on top of whichever plan you're on. It charges you to store and ship your inventory: a fulfillment fee per unit shipped, based on size and weight tier, and a storage fee billed monthly per cubic foot of space your stock occupies. Neither of those changes whether you're on Individual or Professional. Conflating the plan fee with FBA fees is the single most common misunderstanding in this search.

The fees that recur every month, and what drives each one

Five charges show up on a seller's account with any regularity. Two are fixed by your plan choice, three are driven by FBA and your inventory behavior:

  • Selling plan fee — $39.99/month flat (Professional) or $0.99 per item (Individual). Fixed regardless of how FBA performs.
  • Referral fee — a percentage of each sale, 5% to 45% depending on category, with a $0.30 minimum on most categories. Charged per sale, not monthly, but it's the biggest line item most months.
  • FBA fulfillment fee — per unit shipped, set by size tier and weight. Get the dimensional weight wrong and you're paying the wrong tier every single unit.
  • FBA storage fee — billed monthly per cubic foot of space used, with higher rates in the October–December peak window.
  • Aged inventory surcharge — added on top of storage once units sit in a fulfillment center past a set threshold, and it compounds the longer that stock stays put.

A worked example: what a $50 item actually costs

Take a $50 item in the Home and Kitchen category, referral fee 15%. That's a $7.50 referral fee on the sale — real math off Amazon's own published rate, no minimum applies since 15% of $50 clears the $0.30 floor easily.

Now spread the $39.99 Professional plan fee across a month's units. Sell 40 units and it's $1.00 per unit. Sell 400 and it's $0.10 per unit. This is the number most sellers fixate on, and it's the one that matters least at any real volume — it shrinks to a rounding error fast.

What doesn't shrink is the fulfillment fee (tied to that item's size/weight tier — use Amazon's own Revenue Calculator for the exact figure on your dimensions, since it varies and Amazon itself flags its estimates as estimates) and the storage fee, which keeps billing every month that unsold stock sits in a fulfillment center. Across the $500M in Amazon spend Full Circle has managed for 100+ brands, storage and aged-inventory charges are consistently the fee line that grows quietly while sellers are watching the plan fee.

The mistake sellers make chasing the wrong fee

The instinct when margin gets tight is to downgrade from Professional to Individual to "save" the $39.99. For a seller doing under roughly 40 units a month, that math works — $0.99 per item is cheaper than the flat fee. Above that, it costs more, and either way it does nothing to the FBA fulfillment fee, storage fee, or referral fee, which keep charging exactly the same regardless of plan.

We've seen the inverse mistake just as often inside client audits: a team spends an afternoon debating the $39.99 line, while a single slow-moving SKU has been quietly racking up storage and aged-inventory surcharges for eight months. The plan fee is visible and fixed, so it feels like the lever to pull. It almost never is.

When the fee number looks wrong

Sometimes the charge itself is the problem, not your understanding of it. A few things worth checking before you assume the fee is correct:

  • Fulfillment fee looks too high — check the size and weight tier Amazon has assigned the ASIN. Dimensional-weight misclassification is common and it's disputable.
  • Storage fee climbing month over month — that's usually a sign a SKU needs a removal-versus-liquidation decision before the aged-inventory surcharge stacks further.
  • A shipment came in short or damaged — that's a reimbursement claim, not a fee dispute, and it has its own filing window.

Common questions

Is the $39.99 fee the FBA fee?

No. The $39.99/month charge is Amazon's Professional selling plan — payment for the account tier, not for fulfillment. FBA fulfillment fees, storage fees and referral fees are separate charges that apply whether you're on the Individual or Professional plan.

Do I still pay FBA fees if I choose the Individual plan instead of Professional?

Yes. The Individual plan swaps the $39.99/month for $0.99 per item sold — that's a selling-plan choice, not an FBA choice. If you use FBA to store and ship, the fulfillment fee, storage fee and referral fee apply exactly the same either way.

How much is the FBA storage fee per month?

It's billed per cubic foot of space your inventory occupies in the fulfillment center, and the rate changes with the calendar, rising in the October–December peak window. The current rate for your size tier lives on your Seller Central rate card — check it there rather than trusting a number quoted on any article, since it moves.

At what point does the Professional plan become cheaper than Individual?

Do the arithmetic on the two published numbers: $0.99 per item versus $39.99 flat. Divide one by the other and you land around 40 items a month — sell more than that and Professional wins; sell fewer and Individual wins.

What's the biggest FBA monthly cost sellers miss?

Storage on slow-moving stock. The plan fee and referral fee are visible and predictable. Storage fees compound month over month on inventory that isn't selling, and the aged-inventory surcharge stacks on top once units sit too long — that's the one that quietly eats margin.
