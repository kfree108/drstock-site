Three different fees hide under the same search term

Search "Amazon storage fees" and you'll get results for three unrelated things, which is why the top results feel oddly beside the point if you're actually running a seller account.

  • AWS S3 storage pricing — cloud object storage for developers and businesses running infrastructure on Amazon Web Services. Priced per GB by storage class (Standard, Intelligent-Tiering, Glacier, and so on), plus separate charges for requests and data transfer. This has nothing to do with selling on Amazon.
  • Amazon Photos storage — personal photo and video backup for consumers. Prime members get free unlimited full-resolution photo storage plus 5 GB of video; everyone else gets 5 GB total, with paid plans starting at $1.99 a month. Also unrelated to selling.
  • FBA storage fees — what Amazon charges sellers to keep inventory in its fulfillment centers. This is almost certainly what you're actually asking about if you found this page.

The rest of this page is about the third one, because that's the fee that shows up on a seller's P&L and actually moves cash.

How the FBA monthly storage fee is calculated

Amazon charges by cubic foot of space your inventory occupies, measured as the item sits in its packaging — not by unit count and not by weight. The rate depends on your size tier (standard-size vs. oversize) and the month: rates are higher October through December, when warehouse space is tightest, than January through September.

Worked example, using the actual math: a product box measuring 18 x 14 x 10 inches has a volume of 18 × 14 × 10 = 2,520 cubic inches. Divide by 1,728 (cubic inches per cubic foot) and you get 1.458 cubic feet per unit. Hold 500 units in a fulfillment center for a month and that's 729 cubic feet of billable space that month. Multiply that by the current per-cubic-foot rate for your size tier and month — published in Seller Central's Fee Preview, not on any public price list — and you have your monthly storage charge.

We're not printing a dollar rate here because Amazon updates it and varies it by tier and season; anyone who quotes you a flat number without asking your size tier and month is guessing. Pull it from your own Fee Preview report.

The fee that actually costs sellers money: the long-term storage surcharge

The monthly storage fee is background noise compared to the aged-inventory surcharge. Once a unit has sat in a fulfillment center longer than 365 days, Amazon adds a separate per-cubic-foot surcharge on top of the regular monthly fee — not instead of it. It keeps compounding every month until the unit is sold, removed, or liquidated.

Across the $500M-plus in Amazon spend Full Circle has managed for more than 100 brands, the aged-inventory surcharge is consistently one of the fee lines nobody budgeted for. It doesn't announce itself. It just appears as a second charge stacked on a storage fee sellers already knew about, on SKUs that stopped moving months earlier and nobody flagged.

The only real defenses are catching slow movers before day 365 and making a deliberate removal-versus-liquidation call — not letting the calendar make it for you.

The common mistake, including the one we've made

The most expensive mistake is treating the monthly Storage Fees report as background noise instead of a monthly check. A close second: a packaging change that quietly bumps a SKU from standard-size to oversize, which changes the rate tier without anyone re-checking it.

We've missed the removal-versus-liquidation window ourselves on a slow-moving SKU and watched the surcharge compound for a full quarter before anyone caught it. The fix wasn't a smarter forecast — it was a calendar trigger at day 300, well before the 365-day cliff, forcing the removal-or-liquidate decision while there was still time to act on it cheaply.

What to do when the number looks wrong

If a storage charge looks off, work through it in this order:

  • Pull the Storage Fees report in Seller Central and confirm which SKUs and size tiers are driving the charge.
  • Check the product's actual dimensions and weight against what Amazon has on file — dimensional-weight misclassification (wrong box size or weight recorded) is the single most common cause of an inflated size tier.
  • If the measurement is wrong, file a case with corrected dimensions and request the fee be recalculated retroactively.
  • If the fee is correct but the inventory is dead, decide between removal and liquidation before the 365-day mark, not after.

If the leak you're chasing is actually in ad spend — wasted budget, not stuck inventory — that's a different diagnosis. That's what Dr. PPC looks at, not this page.

Neither tells a seller what they're actually paying. If you sell on Amazon and got hit with a storage or aged-inventory charge, the FBA structure above is the one that matters; Dr. Stock exists to catch these before they compound, but the report inside Seller Central is free and it's the first place to look.

Common questions

How much are Amazon storage fees for FBA sellers?

There's no single number — the rate depends on your size tier (standard vs. oversize) and the month (October through December runs higher than January through September). The current per-cubic-foot rate is published in your Seller Central Fee Preview, not on a public price page, so check there rather than trusting a flat figure from a third party.

Why did my Amazon storage fee jump in October?

Amazon raises the per-cubic-foot storage rate for the October through December peak season, when fulfillment center space is scarcest. Nothing about your inventory has to change for the fee to go up — the rate itself moves.

What is the Amazon long-term storage fee?

It's a surcharge added on top of the regular monthly storage fee for any unit that's been in a fulfillment center longer than 365 days. It's billed per cubic foot, like the base fee, and it keeps recurring monthly until the unit is sold, removed, or liquidated.

Can I dispute an Amazon storage fee?

Yes, if the fee is based on wrong dimensions or weight. File a case in Seller Central with corrected measurements and request a recalculation. If the dimensions are correct and the fee is simply the cost of slow-moving stock, the fix is a removal or liquidation decision, not a dispute.

Are AWS S3 fees the same as Amazon FBA storage fees?

No. S3 is cloud data storage priced per GB for developers and businesses running infrastructure on AWS. FBA storage fees are charged to sellers per cubic foot of physical warehouse space their inventory occupies. They share the word "storage" and nothing else.
