What Amazon Actually Calls It Now

The “FBA pick and pack fee” was a real, separate line item on Amazon's fee schedule — until around 2015. Back then, Amazon billed order handling, pick and pack, and weight handling as three distinct charges. Sellers who learned FBA fees in that era, or who found an old calculator still bookmarked, are the ones typing this question today.

Amazon folded all three into a single number now usually called the FBA fulfillment fee (sometimes shown as “fulfillment fee — per unit” in Seller Central reports). It still covers picking the item off the shelf, packing it, weighing it, printing and applying the shipping label, and standard customer service on that order. There's no separate “pick and pack” figure to isolate anymore — it's baked into the one per-unit charge.

So if a calculator, spreadsheet template, or blog post is showing you a standalone pick and pack number next to a standalone weight handling number, it's working from a fee schedule Amazon retired years ago. Use it for historical curiosity, not for pricing a live SKU.

How the Fee Is Actually Built

The current fulfillment fee is set by two inputs: size tier and weight — and weight itself is the greater of two numbers, actual weight or dimensional weight. Amazon runs both calculations and bills whichever is higher.

  • Size tier — determined by the product's length, width, height, and weight together. Amazon groups items into tiers (small standard, large standard, small oversize, and so on); crossing a boundary in any one dimension can move a product to a more expensive tier.
  • Actual weight — the item plus its packaging, on a scale, in pounds.
  • Dimensional weight — a stand-in for actual weight on large, light items, calculated from the box's volume. The standard formula is length × width × height, in inches, divided by 139.
  • Billed weight — whichever of actual or dimensional weight is higher becomes the number Amazon prices the unit against.

None of these four steps involve a separate charge for the act of picking or packing. That labor is priced into the per-unit fee at whatever tier and weight the product lands in — it is not itemized out.

A Worked Example: Same Product, Different Packaging

Take a box measuring 18 × 14 × 8 inches. Its volume is 2,016 cubic inches. Divide by 139 and the dimensional weight comes out to roughly 14.5 lbs. If the product and its packaging weigh 6 lbs on a scale, Amazon doesn't bill the 6 — it bills the 14.5, because dimensional weight is higher.

Now change one thing: the seller switches to a slightly smaller box, 16 × 12 × 7 inches. Volume drops to 1,344 cubic inches, dimensional weight drops to roughly 9.7 lbs. Same product, same actual weight, but a meaningfully lower billed weight — and potentially a lower size tier and a lower fee, because the packaging changed rather than the product.

This is the calculation that matters, not a lookup of a fee that no longer exists. If you want the actual dollar figure for your product today, that comes from Amazon's own Revenue Calculator inside Seller Central, using your real dimensions and weight — not a third-party “pick and pack calculator,” most of which are still running pre-2015 math.

The Mistake That Costs the Most: Stale Dimensions

The most expensive version of this problem isn't misunderstanding the fee name — it's never re-checking the dimensions Amazon has on file. Packaging changes, a supplier swaps a box size for a bulkier multi-piece set, a product gets a thicker insert for a new variant — and nobody updates Amazon, so the fee keeps getting billed against stale measurements, sometimes for months.

Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and dimensional-weight misclassification on exactly this pattern — packaging changed, dimensions never updated — is one of the most common, and most fixable, leaks we find when we go through a fee audit line by line. It rarely announces itself; the fee just quietly sits a tier higher than it should.

The second common mistake is treating the fulfillment fee as fixed once it's confirmed correct. Amazon updates the fee schedule at least annually, sometimes with mid-year changes to specific tiers or the dimensional weight divisor itself. A fee that was right in January can be wrong in July without anyone touching the listing.

When the Number Looks Wrong

If the fee looks off, work it in this order:

  • Re-measure the actual shipped package — not the product spec sheet, the box as it leaves the warehouse, including void fill and inserts.
  • Run it through Amazon's Revenue Calculator with those real numbers and compare to what's actually being billed on a recent settlement report.
  • If Amazon's on-file dimensions are wrong, request a remeasurement through Seller Central — this can lower the tier or the billed weight going forward.
  • If the dimensions are right and the fee still looks wrong, it's usually a tier boundary or a fee-schedule update that changed since you last checked, not an error.

If the number you're actually chasing is cost-per-click or ACOS creeping up, that's not a fulfillment fee question at all — that's an ad account leak, and it belongs with Dr. PPC, not here.

Most sellers can resolve a one-off fee question themselves with Amazon's Revenue Calculator and a tape measure. Common questions

Is there still a separate FBA pick and pack fee?

No. Amazon merged order handling, pick and pack, and weight handling into a single per-unit fulfillment fee years ago. The fee still covers the same work — it's just priced as one number instead of three.

Is there an FBA pick and pack fee calculator?

Not one that reflects Amazon's current fee structure, because the fee itself no longer exists as a standalone line item. Amazon's own Revenue Calculator, using a product's real weight and dimensions, is the accurate way to see the current fulfillment fee. Most standalone “pick and pack calculators” you'll find online are running outdated pre-2015 math.

Why did my fulfillment fee change without me changing my product?

Two usual reasons: Amazon updated the fee schedule or a tier boundary, which happens at least annually, or the packaging around the product changed enough to shift the billed dimensional weight even though the item inside is identical.

What's the difference between the fulfillment fee and the referral fee?

The fulfillment fee pays for FBA to pick, pack, weigh, and ship the order. The referral fee is Amazon's cut of the sale price for using the marketplace, charged as a percentage regardless of who fulfills the order. They're billed separately and calculated on entirely different bases.

How do I dispute a fulfillment fee that looks too high?

Re-measure the actual shipped package yourself, compare it to the dimensions Amazon has on file in Seller Central, and if they don't match, submit a product measurement dispute. If they match and the fee is still higher than expected, check whether the fee schedule or the dimensional weight divisor changed since you last looked.
