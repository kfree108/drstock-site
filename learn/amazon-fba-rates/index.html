There's no single 'FBA rate' — here's what actually makes up the number

"Amazon FBA rates" isn't one number you look up — it's five separate charges that stack per unit, per month, or per shipment. Amazon's own FBA page breaks it into fulfillment costs (picking, packing, shipping, based on the product's price, weight and dimensions), storage costs (charged monthly by the cubic feet your inventory occupies), aged inventory surcharges (for anything sitting more than 181 days), returns processing fees, and removal, disposal or liquidation charges if you pull stock out.

On top of that sits the referral fee — Amazon's category commission, charged whether you use FBA or fulfill it yourself. It isn't technically an FBA fee, but it's part of what you actually keep per sale, so any real "rate" you calculate has to include it.

There is no static rate card that applies to every SKU. Amazon publishes a Revenue Calculator for exactly this reason — the fulfillment fee tier for a garden bed and the fulfillment fee tier for a wallet are not the same tier, and neither is fixed once a year. Rates move when Amazon updates size tiers, when peak-season storage surcharges kick in, or when your product's measured dimensions change.

The fulfillment fee: what actually sets your per-unit rate

The fulfillment fee is set by two things: which size tier your product falls into (standard vs. oversize, then sub-tiers within each) and its weight, including dimensional weight where that applies. A small, dense item like a wallet — the kind of product Ridge sells — sits in a different tier than a bulky, lightweight item like a raised garden bed, the kind Epic Gardening ships. Same fulfillment network, same program, completely different per-unit fee.

The single most common driver of an FBA rate that looks wrong is a size or weight tier that no longer matches the product. Packaging changes, a supplier swaps materials, a variant gets added — and the dimensions Amazon has on file stop matching what's actually shipping. Across the $500M+ in Amazon spend we've managed across 100+ brands, dimensional-weight misclassification is one of the most consistent, checkable leaks we find. It isn't exotic. It's a measurement that drifted and nobody re-verified it.

Storage and aged inventory: the rate that changes with the calendar

Storage fees are charged monthly, per cubic foot of space your inventory occupies, and the rate is higher in the fourth quarter than the rest of the year — Amazon prices for the peak-season squeeze on its warehouses. Oversize inventory pays a different per-cubic-foot rate than standard-size inventory.

Then there's the aged inventory surcharge: anything that's been sitting in a fulfillment center for more than 181 days gets charged extra, on top of standard storage, specifically to push sellers toward clearing slow stock rather than warehousing it indefinitely. This is the fee most sellers discover by accident, usually a quarter after it started.

Amazon Warehousing and Distribution (AWD) is worth knowing about here, separate from FBA proper: it's bulk storage with pay-as-you-go pricing, no seasonal surcharge, and it can feed FBA on a replenishment basis. It doesn't replace FBA — it sits upstream of it.

A worked example — how the pieces stack into one landed cost

Here's how the pieces actually combine, using round numbers to show the mechanics — not Amazon's current published rates, which move often enough that anything printed here would be stale by the time you read it. Pull your own numbers from the Revenue Calculator for the real figure on a real SKU.

Say a unit sells for $24.99. A referral fee (category commission) comes off the top. A fulfillment fee comes off next, set by that item's size and weight tier. A monthly storage charge applies for however long that unit sat in the warehouse before it sold, at whatever the per-cubic-foot rate was for that month. If it sat past 181 days, add the aged inventory surcharge on top of standard storage. If a customer returns it and Amazon covers the return shipping, add the returns processing fee. What's left after all of that is what actually lands in the bank — and it's rarely the number sellers picture when they think "FBA rate."

When the rate looks wrong — and the mistake we've made ourselves

When a rate looks wrong, check the boring thing first: the dimensions and weight Amazon has on file for that ASIN, against what's actually in the box. Repackaging is the usual culprit. We've missed this ourselves — a SKU redesign that shaved packaging weight without anyone updating the listing, so the fee stayed calculated against the old, heavier version for months before it got caught.

If the dimensions are right and the charge still looks off, that's a fee dispute, not a mystery — Amazon has a reimbursement process for charges that don't match the product. If storage and aged-inventory fees are climbing because stock genuinely isn't moving, that's not a fee problem, it's a demand problem, and the fix is removal, liquidation, or a pricing decision — not a dispute ticket.

And if the number that's actually leaking money is cost-per-click or wasted ad spend rather than a warehouse fee, that's a different diagnosis — that's Dr. PPC's territory, not this one.

Common questions

Is there an official Amazon FBA rate card?

Not in the sense of one static PDF that applies to every product. Amazon publishes the fee categories and the logic behind them, but the actual dollar amount depends on your product's size tier, weight, and how long it sits in storage. Use the Revenue Calculator for the current number on a specific SKU rather than any printed rate list, including this one.

How much does Amazon FBA cost per unit?

It's the sum of a fulfillment fee (set by size and weight tier), a share of that month's storage fee, and the referral fee for the category — plus, sometimes, an aged inventory surcharge or a returns processing fee. There's no flat per-unit number that applies across products.

Do Amazon FBA shipping rates change during the year?

Yes. Storage is priced higher in the October–December peak season than the rest of the year, and aged inventory surcharges apply once stock has been sitting for more than 181 days. Amazon also runs new-seller shipping credits through the Partnered Carrier program and Amazon Global Logistics, which change what you pay on inbound shipments in your first months.

How do I check if I'm being charged the correct FBA rate?

Compare the dimensions and weight Amazon has on file for the ASIN against what's actually shipping. Packaging changes and supplier swaps are the most common cause of a size or weight tier drifting away from reality, which quietly changes the fulfillment fee without anyone noticing.

What's the difference between FBA fees and Amazon referral fees?

Referral fees are the category commission Amazon charges on every sale, regardless of fulfillment method. FBA fees are separate — they cover storage, fulfillment, aged inventory, returns processing, and removals — and only apply if you're using Fulfillment by Amazon rather than shipping orders yourself.
