What "Blackline Safety stock" means right now

Blackline Safety Corp traded on the Toronto Stock Exchange under the ticker BLN-T until Francisco Partners took the company private. The transaction closed June 30, 2026, for up to $850 million. From that date the shares stopped trading, and shareholders were converted to cash under the terms of the arrangement they'd voted on two weeks earlier.

If a broker app, screener or AI summary is showing you a price that still looks live, it isn't. One cached source shows a final print around $9.05–$9.06 a share, with the open at $9.06 — that's the last trade before the stock came off the board, not a current quote. It will not move again.

The numbers that made BLN-T worth buying

Blackline's own investor materials, published before the deal closed, described a business built on wearable gas detectors and connected safety hardware sold with a subscription attached — what the company called Hardware-enabled Software-as-a-Service. The headline figures: 37 consecutive quarters of year-over-year revenue growth, an 83% service gross margin, $93.0 million in annual recurring revenue, and 123% net dollar retention, meaning existing customers were spending more each year, not less.

Thirty-seven straight quarters is more than nine years without a single year-over-year revenue decline — through a pandemic and a rate cycle that hurt plenty of industrial hardware names. The company sold through roughly 250 global distributors into a market it sized at $17.7 billion for connected gas detection, and had generated more than $500 million in total revenue since its flagship product line launched in 2017.

None of that guarantees $850 million was the right price — shareholder opposition to the deal was public before the vote passed — but it explains why a private equity buyer showed up with a check at all.

How the deal actually moved from announcement to delisting

The path from first announcement to the stock coming off the exchange ran just under three months. Each step removed a condition the deal needed to close, and each one is the kind of milestone that moves a "maybe" quote toward a firm delisting date.

The mistake most searches make: two different companies named Blackline

A lot of the traffic on this query is looking for the wrong company. Blackline Safety Corp (TSX: BLN-T) makes wearable gas detectors and connected safety hardware for industrial workers. Blackline Inc. (Nasdaq: BL) is an unrelated financial close and accounting automation software company, headquartered in the US. They share a name and nothing else — different products, different exchanges, different investors, and now, different market status, since Blackline Safety is private and Blackline Inc. is not.

If you typed "blackline stock" or "blackline safety stock" hoping for a live quote and landed on numbers that don't match what you remembered, check which company the page is actually about before assuming the data is wrong.

If you're still holding shares, or your app still shows a price

If you were a BLN-T shareholder on the record date, your shares were converted to cash under the arrangement — check the transaction confirmation from your broker or the transfer agent, not a stock screener, for the actual amount and timing. The Globe and Mail's own listing continues showing historical price and volume data because news wires and price feeds don't always delist a ticker's page the moment trading stops; the numbers freeze, they don't update.

If a source is telling you BLN-T is still tradable, or quoting a price after June 30, 2026, that source is wrong. Go to TSX's own delisting notices or the company's SEDAR+ filings for the authoritative record, not a cached financial aggregator.

Common questions

Is Blackline Safety Corp still publicly traded?

No. Francisco Partners completed its take-private acquisition on June 30, 2026, and BLN-T stopped trading on the Toronto Stock Exchange that day. There is no live market price to check anymore.

What's the difference between Blackline Safety and Blackline Inc.?

Blackline Safety Corp (TSX: BLN-T) made wearable gas detectors and connected safety hardware and is now private. Blackline Inc. (Nasdaq: BL) is a separate, unrelated company that makes financial close and accounting software. Searches for one often surface results for the other.

What happened to my BLN-T shares after the deal closed?

Under the arrangement, shareholders received cash for their shares once the transaction closed. The specific per-share amount and payment timing would be in the arrangement circular and your broker's confirmation — check those directly rather than a price screener.

Why does some site still show a BLN-T price ticking?

Financial data aggregators often cache the last available quote rather than actively flagging a delisting. A number that looks live, including one showing a last print near $9.05–$9.06, is a frozen historical print, not a current market price.
