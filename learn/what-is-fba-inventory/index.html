What FBA Inventory Actually Is

FBA inventory is every unit of your product that you have sent to Amazon under Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA), from the moment it is on its way to the moment a customer buys it. Amazon holds it, handles the orders and shipping, and deals with customer service on those orders. Ownership does not change: the units are yours, they sit on your balance sheet, and you pay for the space they take up.

That last point is what most definitions skip. FBA inventory is not just a number in Seller Central. It is cash you spent on product, freight and prep that is now sitting on someone else's shelf, waiting to turn back into revenue. How fast it turns is the real subject of inventory management.

The Statuses Your FBA Inventory Moves Through

Amazon splits your stock into statuses, and each one means something different for sales and for cash. The names below match what you see in Seller Central's inventory views; check Amazon's own help pages for the current wording, because Amazon revises its labels from time to time.

  • Inbound: units you have shipped to Amazon that have not yet been checked in. They cannot be bought yet, so a long check-in delay is lost sales.
  • Fulfillable (sellable): units on the shelf and available to customers. This is the number that decides whether you stay in stock.
  • Reserved: units set aside for open orders, transfers between buildings, or processing. They are yours but temporarily not for sale.
  • Unfulfillable: units Amazon cannot sell, usually because they are damaged, expired, defective or flagged. You can ask for them to be returned or disposed of, and some may qualify for reimbursement.

The mistake to avoid is reading your total as your sellable stock. A SKU can show a healthy total while most of it is inbound or reserved, and you can still go out of stock.

How Amazon Receives and Stores It

The path is the same for most sellers. You create a shipping plan, label and prep your units to Amazon's requirements, send them to the fulfillment centers Amazon assigns, and Amazon checks them in and makes them available. Amazon decides where your stock is stored, and a single SKU can be split across several buildings.

Three things follow from that:

  • Check-in time is part of your lead time. Units that are in transit or waiting to be received are not yet earning.
  • Prep errors cost you. Mislabelled or poorly packed units can be delayed or end up unfulfillable.
  • Your storage bill follows your stock. Amazon charges for the space your inventory uses, and adds surcharges on stock that sits for a long time. The exact rates change, so read Amazon's current FBA fee pages before you plan a purchase order.

Why FBA Inventory Is a Cash Problem, Not Just a Stock Problem

Having too little and too much both cost money, and both are easy to miss in a sales dashboard.

  • Too little: you stock out, ads keep spending on a listing that cannot sell, and organic rank can slip while you are out.
  • Too much: cash is trapped in slow-moving units while storage fees and aged-inventory surcharges build up.
  • Wrong place or wrong status: stock is counted but not sellable, so the number looks fine and the listing still goes dark.

A worked example, with made-up round numbers. Say a SKU has 1,200 units in FBA and sells 40 a day. That is 1,200 ÷ 40 = 30 days of cover. If your lead time, from placing the purchase order to units becoming sellable, is 35 days, you are already 5 days late, even though the dashboard shows 1,200 units on hand. Now flip it: if the same SKU sold only 10 a day, 1,200 units would be 120 days of cover, and most of that money would sit on the shelf for months.

A Checklist for Reading Your Own FBA Inventory

Run this on your top SKUs once a week. It takes minutes and catches most surprises.

  • Days of cover: sellable units divided by average daily sales. Compare it with your real lead time, not the supplier's quoted one.
  • Status split: how much of the total is inbound, reserved or unfulfillable?
  • Inbound age: any shipment that has been in transit or unreceived for longer than normal needs a look.
  • Slow movers: SKUs with far more cover than the rest are the ones that will collect storage and aged-inventory charges.
  • Lost and damaged units: compare what you sent against what was received and what was sold. Gaps may be reimbursable, and Amazon's claim windows are limited, so check the current rules.
  • Fee errors: check that the size and weight tier Amazon has on file for each product matches reality, because it drives the fees on every unit.

FBA Inventory vs Other Terms You'll Hear

The word gets used loosely, so here is how it differs from its neighbors:

  • FBM inventory (fulfilled by merchant) is stock you hold and ship yourself or through a 3PL. It does not sit in Amazon's buildings.
  • The FBA Inventory report in Seller Central is a data export of your FBA stock by SKU and status. It is a record of FBA inventory, not a different kind.
  • Amazon Business Restock is a B2B supply service for companies buying for their own worksites. It has nothing to do with your FBA account.

Common questions

What is FBA inventory?

It is the stock you own that has been sent to Amazon's fulfillment centers under Fulfillment by Amazon. Amazon stores it and ships orders for you, but the units stay yours until they sell.

What is the difference between fulfillable and unfulfillable FBA inventory?

Fulfillable units are in good condition and available for customers to buy. Unfulfillable units are damaged, expired, defective or otherwise cannot be sold, and you can have them returned or disposed of. Some may be eligible for reimbursement.

Who owns FBA inventory?

You do. Amazon holds it and charges you for storage and fulfillment, but ownership stays with the seller until a customer buys it. That is why stock sitting too long is a cost to you, not to Amazon.

How do I check my FBA inventory?

Open the Inventory section of Seller Central and look at your FBA inventory views, or download the FBA Inventory report. Look at the status split, not just the total, so you can see what is sellable now versus inbound or reserved.

How much FBA inventory should I keep?

Enough to cover your real lead time plus a safety buffer, and no more. For example, a SKU selling 40 units a day with a 35-day lead time and 15 days of safety stock needs 40 × 35 + 40 × 15 = 2,000 units sellable when you place the next order. Recheck it as sales speed and lead times change.
