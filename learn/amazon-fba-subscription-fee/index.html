Is There Really an Amazon FBA Subscription Fee?

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1413 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon fba subscription fee”
The short answer

There is no separate FBA subscription fee. The $39.99/month charge is Amazon's Professional selling plan, which most FBA sellers carry. FBA itself bills per-unit fulfillment fees, per-cubic-foot storage fees, and the standard referral fee — none of them flat or monthly.

What's Actually Being Called a 'Subscription Fee'

Search this term and you'll usually land on Amazon's own Professional selling plan, which costs $39.99 a month. That's real money, but it isn't an FBA fee — it's the cost of having a Professional seller account at all, whether you fulfill orders yourself or use FBA. The Individual plan charges $0.99 per item sold instead of the monthly fee, aimed at sellers listing a handful of products.

FBA itself doesn't have a subscription. It charges you every time a unit sells or sits in a warehouse: a referral fee on the sale, a fulfillment fee per unit shipped, and a storage fee per cubic foot per month. None of those are flat monthly charges — they scale with what you sell and how much space it takes up.

The Real Fee Stack Once You Add FBA

Once FBA is switched on, four separate charges apply to the same unit, from different rulebooks:

  • Selling plan fee — $0.99/item (Individual) or $39.99/month (Professional). Charged for having a seller account, not for using FBA.
  • Referral fee — a percentage of the item's total price, set by category, with a minimum amount if the percentage works out lower. Amazon's published table runs from around 8% in categories like electronics up to 20% in categories like fine art and gift cards, with a $0.30 minimum in most categories.
  • FBA fulfillment fee — charged per unit shipped from an Amazon warehouse, based on the size and weight tier of the item once Amazon measures and weighs it.
  • FBA storage fee — charged per cubic foot per month for inventory sitting in an Amazon warehouse, higher in October through December, with an added aged-inventory surcharge once units sit past Amazon's aging thresholds.

The subscription fee is the only flat, predictable number on that list. Everything else moves with your catalog, your sell-through, and the season.

A Worked Example

Say you sell a $25 kitchen gadget under Home and Kitchen, where the referral fee is 15% with a $0.30 minimum. Amazon takes the greater of the two: 15% of $25 is $3.75, well above the $0.30 floor, so the referral fee on that sale is $3.75.

Now add the selling plan fee. On the Professional plan, $39.99/month spread across, say, 200 units sold that month works out to roughly $0.20 per unit — small, but real. Then add the FBA fulfillment fee for that item's specific size and weight tier, and the storage fee for however many cubic feet it occupied while it waited to sell. Amazon's own Revenue Calculator is the only place to get those two numbers accurately for your exact product, because they change by size tier and by month — anyone quoting you a flat fulfillment fee without your dimensions is guessing.

The Mistake Most New Sellers Make

The most common budgeting error isn't misreading the referral fee table — it's treating the $39.99 subscription and the referral fee as the whole cost, and forgetting fulfillment and storage entirely. On a low-margin item, fulfillment and storage fees together often outweigh the referral fee, especially for anything bulky or slow-moving.

The second mistake is trusting Amazon's fee assessment without checking it. Dimensional-weight misclassification — where Amazon measures a box wrong and bumps it into a more expensive size tier — is one of the most consistent, checkable leaks we see. Across the more than $500M in Amazon spend Full Circle has managed for 100+ brands, fee errors of exactly this kind show up often enough that they're worth a standing check, not a one-time audit.

When the Fee Looks Wrong

If a fulfillment fee jumps between two otherwise identical shipments, don't assume it's correct because Amazon issued it. Pull the item's actual dimensions and weight, compare them against the tier Amazon billed, and file a case if they don't match — dimensional-weight disputes are one of the few fee categories Amazon will actually reverse when you can show the measurement was wrong.

If storage fees spike, check whether units crossed an aging threshold before you could sell through them — that's an inventory planning problem, not a fee dispute, and the fix is reorder timing, not an appeal. And if the number that's actually bothering you is rising ACOS or wasted ad spend, that's not a fee question at all — that's a job for whoever runs the ad account, not the fulfillment side.

Common questions

Is the $39.99 monthly fee an FBA fee?

No. It's the Professional selling plan fee, charged for having that type of seller account. You'd pay it whether you fulfill orders yourself or use FBA. FBA's own charges — fulfillment and storage — are billed separately, per unit and per cubic foot.

Can I use FBA on the $0.99 Individual plan?

Amazon's own plan comparison lists FBA as an available tool without clearly gating it to one tier in the current text, but plan inclusions shift over time — check the live comparison chart before assuming. Either way, the $0.99-vs-$39.99 charge is for the selling plan, not the fulfillment method.

Why do referral fees and FBA fulfillment fees both apply to the same sale?

They pay for different things. The referral fee covers access to Amazon's marketplace and customer base and applies no matter who fulfills the order. The FBA fulfillment fee covers the physical pick, pack and ship work, and only applies if you use FBA. Fulfill orders yourself and you skip that second charge.

What's the biggest hidden cost sellers miss when budgeting for FBA?

Storage and aged-inventory surcharges. Most sellers plan for the referral fee and the fulfillment fee because those show up per sale. Storage fees accrue quietly on unsold inventory, and the aged-inventory surcharge only shows up once units have sat too long — by which point it's a planning fix, not a fee dispute.

Where do I check the exact fees for my product?

Amazon's Revenue Calculator gives the fulfillment fee for your item's actual dimensions and weight, and the category referral fee table gives the percentage and minimum. Both change by product and by month, so a number quoted in a blog post or listicle is a starting estimate, not your real fee.
