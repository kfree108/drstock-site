What Stranded Inventory Actually Means

Stranded inventory is FBA stock that Amazon is holding for you but cannot sell, because there is no active listing attached to it. The units are physically in a fulfillment center. They are checked in, counted and on your inventory report. What is missing is a live offer a customer could buy.

That makes it different from the other kinds of unsellable stock. Damaged or expired units have a problem with the item. Aged inventory has a problem with time. Stranded inventory has a problem with the listing, which is why the fix is almost always an admin task rather than a logistics one.

The costs are quiet. Stranded units earn nothing, they still take up space, and the cash you paid for them is stuck. Because nothing flashes red on your sales dashboard, they can sit for weeks before anyone looks.

Why Inventory Gets Stranded

The cause is nearly always one of a short list. Knowing which one you have decides the fix.

  • The listing was closed or deleted. Someone removed the offer, or the ASIN was deleted, while units were still inbound or in stock.
  • The listing is suppressed. A missing required attribute, an image that fails Amazon's standards, or a category requirement you have not met can take the offer offline.
  • The listing has an error. A mismatch between the product and its listing data, or a problem with the SKU, can leave stock with nowhere to attach.
  • A compliance or approval gate. Restricted categories and required documents can block an offer until you submit what Amazon asks for.
  • A relist under a new ASIN or SKU. If you rebuild a listing instead of reviving the old one, the stock stays attached to the old identifier.
  • A price or setting problem. An offer that is inactive because of a pricing rule or a quantity setting can look like a stranded unit.

Seller Central usually states a reason next to each stranded item. Treat that text as your starting point, then confirm it on the listing itself. If the stated reason is vague, the Seller Support case route is the way to get a specific answer in writing.

How to Find It in Seller Central

Amazon provides a dedicated Stranded Inventory view in the Inventory section of Seller Central. It lists each affected SKU, the number of units and the reason Amazon gives. Menu names move around from time to time, so if you do not see it where you expect, search Seller Central for "stranded inventory".

A short routine that works:

  • Open the stranded inventory view and export the list.
  • Sort by units, so the biggest cash tie-ups come first.
  • Note the stated reason for each row.
  • Group rows by cause: listing closed, listing suppressed, listing error, approval needed.
  • Work one group at a time, because one fix often clears many SKUs.

Run this on a schedule instead of waiting for a problem. Stranded stock is much cheaper to fix in the week it appears than after a quarter of quiet storage fees.

How to Fix It: Relist, Repair or Remove

You have three routes, and the right one depends on whether the product still has a future.

  • Repair the listing. If the listing is suppressed or has an error, fix the missing attribute, image or approval, then confirm the offer is active. Once an active offer exists for that SKU, the units become sellable again.
  • Relist. If the offer was closed or deleted, create the offer again against the same SKU where you can. Using the original SKU matters, because the stranded units are tied to it.
  • Remove. If the product is discontinued, or fixing it costs more than the stock is worth, create a removal order to have the units returned to you or disposed of. Do this deliberately, and before Amazon's own cleanup rules decide for you.

Before any removal, check one more thing: whether the units are actually lost or damaged inside the warehouse. Inventory that Amazon misplaced is a reimbursement question, separate from the stranded-listing question. Reconcile the units on your reports first, so you do not remove or write off stock you could claim for.

Do not try to work around a listing block with a duplicate listing or other tricks. They can put your account at risk, and the stranded stock stays stranded. If Amazon is blocking an offer, fix the stated reason or remove the stock.

What Stranded Inventory Costs You

There are three costs, and only one of them shows up on a fee line.

  • Storage fees. Units in the fulfillment center are still taking up space, so storage charges can keep accruing even though nothing can sell. Check your own fee reports for the current rates, since they change by season and by size tier.
  • Lost sales. If the SKU was a seller, every day it is stranded is a day of missing revenue and, for a competitive listing, lost ranking momentum.
  • Trapped cash. The money you paid your supplier is sitting in stock you cannot sell. For a small brand this is often the largest cost, because it limits what you can reorder.

A simple way to size it: for each stranded SKU, multiply units by your landed cost per unit. That is the cash tied up. Add up the column, and you have the number to compare against the effort of fixing the listings.

How to Stop It Happening Again

Most stranded inventory is created by routine changes, not by Amazon. A listing edit, a catalog cleanup or a rushed relist leaves units behind. These habits prevent most of it:

  • Never close or delete a listing while stock is inbound or on hand. Check inventory first.
  • Keep one owner for listing changes. Stranded stock tends to appear when several people edit the catalog and no one reconciles it.
  • Check the stranded view weekly. Put it on the same list as your inventory health and ad checks.
  • Watch listing suppression alerts. A suppressed listing with stock on hand is a stranded unit waiting to happen.
  • Link it to your reorder process. Do not send a purchase order for a SKU whose listing is in trouble.

Common questions

What is stranded inventory on Amazon?

It is FBA stock in an Amazon fulfillment center with no active listing, so customers cannot buy it. It is usually caused by a closed, deleted or suppressed listing, or a listing error.

How do I fix stranded inventory on Amazon?

Open the stranded inventory view in Seller Central, read the stated reason, and fix it. That may mean repairing a suppressed listing, recreating the offer on the same SKU, or submitting approval documents. If the product is finished, create a removal order.

Do I pay storage fees on stranded inventory?

The units are still taking up space in the fulfillment center, so storage charges can continue. Check your own fee reports for the exact rates that apply to you.

Why is my Amazon inventory stranded with no clear reason?

The reason shown can be vague. Check whether the listing is active, suppressed or recently edited, and whether the SKU matches the one holding the stock. If it is still unclear, open a Seller Support case and ask for the specific cause in writing.

Can I get stranded inventory back from Amazon?

Yes. Create a removal order for the units and Amazon will return them to you or dispose of them, depending on what you choose. Reconcile your inventory first, in case any units are lost or damaged and eligible for reimbursement.
