How Often Does Amazon Restock?

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1444 words · Written against what currently ranked for “how often does amazon restock”
The short answer

Amazon has no fixed restock schedule. Items get restocked whenever the seller, vendor, or Amazon's own buying team pushes in new inventory and it gets checked into the warehouse — which can be hours for a fast-moving FBA seller, or months for a manufacturer-constrained item like a PS5 or a Labubu blind box.

There's No Schedule — Here's What Actually Controls It

"Restocked" just means new units arrived at a warehouse and got checked into inventory. Nothing on Amazon runs on a calendar. What decides the timing is who owns the inventory and how fast they can move it.

  • FBA sellers control their own reorder timing. Once they place a purchase order and ship it to Amazon, the restock happens the day it's received and processed at the fulfillment center.
  • FBM (merchant-fulfilled) sellers restock on their own warehouse clock — Amazon has no visibility or role in it at all.
  • Vendor / Amazon Retail items (where Amazon itself buys and resells stock) restock on Amazon's own purchase order cadence to that brand, which the brand doesn't control.
  • Manufacturer-allocated drops — PS5s, Labubu blind boxes, anything Pop Mart sells through Amazon — restock whenever the manufacturer decides to send inventory into that channel, which has nothing to do with Amazon's systems at all.

That's also why "Temporarily out of stock" and "Currently unavailable" mean different things. The first is a live listing waiting on a shipment already in motion. The second usually means the seller pulled the offer, and there's no shipment to wait for — restocking that one depends entirely on whether the seller relists it.

The Math Behind Every Restock (Worked Example)

Across the 70+ brands live in the Full Circle and reMKTR group, the same three numbers decide every restock we see: velocity, lead time, and safety stock. Once you know those three, you can predict the gap before it happens.

Say a seller moves 40 units a day of a bestseller, and it takes 35 days for a new shipment to land at the fulfillment center once it's ordered. The reorder point sits around 1,400 units (40 × 35). Add a safety-stock buffer for demand swings — call it another 300 units — and the real trigger is 1,700 units on hand. If the seller reorders the moment stock hits 1,700, the next restock lands roughly five weeks later with no visible gap on the listing at all.

Miss that trigger by even a week — reorder at 1,000 units instead of 1,700 — and the listing runs dry with 25 days of lead time still on the clock. That's the entire mechanism behind every "how long until it's back" question. It's arithmetic, not a mystery, and it's set (or missed) by a human weeks before the stockout ever shows up on the page.

Why PS5 and Labubu / Pop Mart Restocks Feel Random

They feel random because they are, in a specific sense: the constraint isn't Amazon's system, it's the manufacturer's production and allocation. A console maker or a collectible brand decides how many units go to Amazon versus their own site, retail stores, and other channels. Amazon just sells whatever gets allocated to it.

Pop Mart is the clearest example. It sells through its own storefront, physical retail, and third-party Amazon sellers, and it decides how much inventory each channel gets. So "how often does Pop Mart restock on Amazon" doesn't have a cadence answer — it has an allocation answer, and that allocation can change month to month based on what Pop Mart chooses to prioritize. A blind-box drop that sells out in an hour isn't an Amazon inventory failure; it's a demand-versus-allocation mismatch decided somewhere else entirely.

PS5 works the same way. Sony sets production and channel allocation; Amazon restocks the moment its allotted units arrive, and that can be days or months depending on chip supply and how many other retailers are also waiting on the same production run.

When the Answer Is Bad News

If you're a shopper: stock-alert services and browser extensions genuinely help, but be clear-eyed about their limits — most of them are refreshing the public listing page, not reading Amazon's actual inventory feed, so they can lag or miss restocks entirely. There's no way to guarantee timing on a manufacturer-constrained item, no matter what a rebate site or alert tool implies.

If you're a seller and your own listing is the one going dark: the fix is almost never "Amazon's system." It's your reorder point. The most common mistake — and one we've made ourselves on a client account — is calculating the reorder trigger off a trailing average instead of peak velocity during the lead-time window. We once had a SKU set on a 90-day trailing average that didn't account for a seasonal spike in the middle of that window; the listing went out of stock two weeks before the replenishment shipment landed, right in the middle of an ad campaign, and the rank drop took weeks to recover after restock.

If the real problem is that ad spend is being wasted chasing a listing that's about to stock out — that's not an inventory fix, that's a campaign-pacing fix, and it belongs with whoever runs the PPC account (Dr. PPC territory), not the warehouse side.

Common questions

How often does Amazon restock PS5?

There's no schedule. Sony allocates a batch of consoles to Amazon among other retailers based on production and channel priorities, and Amazon lists them the moment that allocation arrives. Gaps have run from days to several months depending on chip supply and demand that quarter.

How often does Amazon restock Labubu items?

Same mechanism as PS5, different manufacturer. Pop Mart decides how many units go to its Amazon storefront versus its own site and retail stores, and blind-box drops can sell out within an hour of restocking. There's no fixed interval to plan around.

Does Pop Mart restock on Amazon on a regular cycle?

No published cycle exists. Restocking on Amazon depends on how much of Pop Mart's total production Pop Mart chooses to route to that channel in a given period, which can shift month to month with demand and other channel priorities.

What does 'Temporarily out of stock' mean versus 'Currently unavailable'?

Temporarily out of stock means a shipment is already in motion and the listing is waiting on it — this is the case where a restock is genuinely coming. Currently unavailable usually means the seller pulled the offer, and there's no shipment behind it, so it may never come back on that listing.

As a seller, how do I know if my restock timing is actually broken?

Check whether your reorder point was calculated on peak velocity during your real lead-time window, not a trailing average. If a SKU has stocked out more than once while sales were flat or growing, the trigger is set too low for the lead time you're actually getting from your supplier — that's a math problem, fixable before the next cycle.
