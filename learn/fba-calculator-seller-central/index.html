Where the FBA calculator actually lives in Seller Central

Nobody calls it the FBA calculator inside the account itself. It's the Revenue Calculator, and you reach it by searching "Revenue Calculator" in the Seller Central search bar or going to Growth > FBA Revenue Calculator in the left nav. There's also a public version at sellercentral.amazon.com/fba/revenuecalculator that doesn't require login — useful for research, but it won't pull your own catalog history.

It works one ASIN at a time. Type in a listing — yours or a competitor's — and it pulls the current referral fee percentage, size tier, and fulfillment fee for that category. Add your cost of goods and inbound shipping, and it shows net proceeds under FBA versus FBM side by side on the same screen. That direct comparison is the main reason people search for a "seller FBA calculator" instead of building the spreadsheet themselves.

What it doesn't do: track what Amazon actually charged you last month, flag a size-tier reclassification that happened mid-quarter, or run the same math across a 200-SKU catalog at once. Free browser-based calculators from research tools fill some of that gap with ASIN auto-fill and multi-scenario toggles — genuinely handy for pricing a new product before you list it. But they're pulling the same published fee schedule Amazon publishes, not your settlement data, so they inherit the same blind spot.

A worked example: what the numbers actually do

Take a $19.99 kitchen item — a cast-iron trivet in roughly the size and weight range HexClad's cookware sits in — shipped FBA as Large Standard at 1.2 lb. Enter the sale price, category, and your landed cost, and the Revenue Calculator walks through the same chain any FBA seller needs to run before setting a price.

  • Sale price, minus the referral fee for the category (Kitchen sits around 15%)
  • Minus the FBA fulfillment fee for that size tier and weight
  • Minus a prorated monthly storage charge based on cubic feet held
  • Minus your cost of goods and inbound freight
  • What's left is net profit per unit, and margin as a percentage of sale price

The table below shows that math end to end. Notice what's missing from the default screen: ad spend and a returns reserve. The calculator assumes you'll account for those separately — it's answering "what does Amazon take," not "what's my true bottom line after marketing." If you stop at the calculator's number and price to that margin, you're pricing to a number that's already too generous.

When the number is wrong — three things to check first

The most common gap is dimensional weight. The calculator uses the dimensions you type in, not the ones Amazon's fulfillment center measures when the box actually arrives. If your packaging changed, or you estimated instead of measuring the taped, sealed carton, Amazon can reclassify the size tier and charge more than the estimate showed — and that only shows up in your settlement report, weeks later. Across the accounts Full Circle has managed more than $500M in revenue across 100+ brands, dimensional misclassification is one of the most consistent leaks we find, and it never shows up in a pre-launch calculator run.

Second, check whether low-inventory-level fees or the aged-inventory surcharge are toggled on. Both are real 2025/2026-era fees tied to your own stock levels and cover-weeks, not the listing itself, so a calculator run in isolation — without your current inventory position — will simply omit them.

Third, confirm the referral fee category matches what Amazon actually applies. Category assignment drives the referral rate, and a miscategorized listing will quote you the wrong percentage on every single sale, not just the one you're checking.

The mistake almost everyone makes with this calculator

The mistake is running it once, at launch, and treating that number as permanent. Referral rates, fulfillment fee tiers, and storage rates change on Amazon's schedule, not yours — storage pricing alone typically steps up for the October-to-December peak window. A margin that worked in March can be gone by November on the exact same SKU, same price, same cost of goods.

We've made a version of this mistake ourselves: assuming a Revenue Calculator projection would hold up against the real settlement once volume scaled. It didn't, on a SKU where the measured carton came in a size tier above what was entered at listing setup. The fix wasn't a better calculator — it was reconciling the projection against actual settlement data on a schedule, not a one-time check.

Where this fits versus tracking it on an ongoing basis

The Revenue Calculator, and free tools like it, are the right tool for a specific job: deciding whether a product is worth listing, and at what price, before you commit inventory dollars. That's a pricing decision, made once, on paper. Use it there.

It's the wrong tool for an ongoing question: are the fees you're actually being charged, across a real catalog, matching what they should be — and if not, is that recoverable. That's not a calculator problem, it's a reconciliation problem, and it only shows up when you compare projected fees against settlement reports SKU by SKU, month by month. Which one you should actually pick

Use Seller Central's Revenue Calculator, or a free third-party version, to price a product before you list it — that's what it's built for. Once you're live with real volume, the question shifts from projected fees to actual ones, and that needs settlement-level reconciliation, not another calculator run.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Total three numbers first: storage and aged-inventory surcharges for the last twelve months, lost sales on days your best sellers were out of stock, and cash sitting in SKUs that have not moved in 180 days. Then ask each vendor what they would do about those three in week one.

Common questions

Is the FBA calculator in Seller Central free?

Yes. It's built into every Seller Central account under the Revenue Calculator, with no separate fee, and there's also a public version that doesn't require login for browsing before you have a seller account.

Why doesn't my Revenue Calculator estimate match my actual settlement report?

The two most common reasons are dimensional weight and toggled fees. The calculator uses the dimensions you entered, not the measured carton, and Amazon can reclassify the size tier on arrival. It also won't include low-inventory-level or aged-inventory surcharges unless you know to check for them separately against your current stock position.

Does the calculator include storage and aged-inventory fees automatically?

The default view covers referral and fulfillment fees. Storage, low-inventory-level, and aged-inventory surcharges are tied to your current inventory levels and cover-weeks, not the listing, so they need to be checked against your actual stock position rather than assumed from a single calculator run.

Can I use it to compare FBA versus FBM on the same product?

Yes, that side-by-side comparison is the main reason to use Amazon's own tool over building the math from scratch. Enter your FBM shipping cost and it shows net proceeds under both fulfillment methods for the same ASIN.

What's the real difference between this and third-party FBA calculators?

Amazon's Revenue Calculator and most free third-party tools both run on the published fee schedule, one ASIN at a time. Some third-party tools add ASIN auto-fill and catalog-wide views, which helps at the research stage. None of them reconcile against your actual settlement data — that's a separate, ongoing check, not a calculator feature.
