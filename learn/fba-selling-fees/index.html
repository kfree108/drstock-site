The short answer

FBA selling fees are three or four separate charges stacked together: a selling plan fee ($0.99/item or $39.99/month), a referral fee (a category percentage of the sale price, $0.30 minimum), and an FBA fulfillment fee plus storage fees set by size, weight and how long inventory sits.

What You're Actually Paying For

Every FBA sale carries at least three, and usually four, separate charges. They come from different parts of Amazon's business and they're calculated differently, which is why "what does FBA cost" doesn't have a one-number answer.

  • Selling plan fee: $0.99 per item on the Individual plan, or a flat $39.99/month on Professional, regardless of volume.
  • Referral fee: a percentage of the total sale price (item plus shipping and any gift wrap), set by product category, with a $0.30 minimum on most categories.
  • FBA fulfillment fee: what Amazon charges to pick, pack and ship the order, driven by size tier and weight — including dimensional weight, which is where most fee disputes actually start.
  • Storage fee: a monthly charge per cubic foot of warehouse space, higher in the fourth quarter, with an added aged-inventory surcharge for stock that sits too long.

Referral fee percentages range from 5% on some clothing under $15 up to 45% on Amazon Device Accessories, with most categories landing between 8% and 17%. That spread is worth checking against your own catalog before you assume one flat rate applies everywhere — it usually doesn't.

A Worked Example: A $40 Home and Kitchen Item

Take a $40 item in the Home and Kitchen category, sold through FBA on a Professional plan.

  • Referral fee: Home and Kitchen sits at a flat 15%, no tiering. 15% of $40 is $6.00 — well above the $0.30 minimum, so $6.00 is what actually gets deducted.
  • Selling plan fee: $39.99/month, spread across however many units sell that month. At low volume this is the piece people forget to allocate per unit before comparing costs to Individual.
  • FBA fulfillment fee: set by size and weight tier, not by category or price. Two $40 products can carry different fulfillment fees because one measures half an inch larger, or its dimensional weight calculation lands in a different bracket. Run the specific ASIN through Amazon's Revenue Calculator rather than assuming a category average — that's the one place the estimate is genuinely worth trusting.
  • Storage fee: charged per cubic foot per month, separate from whether the unit sells. It accrues on the shelf whether or not a customer buys it that month.

Add the referral fee, the fulfillment fee, a per-unit share of the monthly plan fee, and a per-unit share of storage, and that's the true cost of the sale — not the referral fee alone, which is the number most people quote when they ask what FBA costs.

The Mistakes That Quietly Cost Sellers Money

The referral fee usually isn't the problem — sellers copy that number off Amazon's own page correctly often enough. It's the fulfillment fee, because it's driven by size tier and dimensional weight, and both change quietly. Repackage a product, switch a supplier, or let Amazon re-measure a returned unit, and the size tier can shift without a notification that says so plainly.

Across managing more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, the same handful of leaks show up repeatedly: a product bumped into a larger size tier after an unflagged packaging change, storage fees left running on a SKU that stopped selling months ago, and referral fee category mismatches — an item sitting in a general category paying a higher rate than the specific one it actually qualifies for.

We've made a version of this mistake too: treating a Revenue Calculator estimate as permanent instead of re-checking it after a packaging or supplier change. The calculator is a snapshot. It's only as accurate as the dimensions and weight you feed it on the day you run it, not a standing guarantee for the life of the product.

When the Fee Looks Wrong: What to Actually Do

If a fee looks higher than expected — higher than the calculator estimated, higher than last month with no obvious reason — don't absorb it as a cost of doing business. Start with the size tier: check the dimensions and weight Amazon currently has on file for that ASIN against what you actually shipped. Dimensional-weight misclassification is fixable, but only if someone catches it and files the correction.

If the fee was genuinely charged wrong — wrong tier, wrong category, a fulfillment fee applied to a unit that was actually lost or damaged in an Amazon warehouse — that's a reimbursement case, not something to write off in a support ticket. Amazon has a defined process and a filing window; the reimbursement doesn't appear automatically just because the error happened.

If a fix doesn't hold and the same fee reappears next month, check whether the correction actually changed the underlying product record and size tier, or just adjusted one invoice. A one-time credit doesn't stop a recurring misclassification — the record has to change or the same fee shows up again next cycle.

Common questions

What's the difference between the referral fee and the FBA fulfillment fee?

The referral fee is Amazon's commission for the sale itself — a percentage of the price, set by product category. The fulfillment fee is separate: it's what Amazon charges to physically pick, pack and ship the order, and it's driven by the product's size and weight tier, not its category or price.

Do I still pay FBA fees if the item doesn't sell?

You pay storage fees every month an item sits in an Amazon warehouse, whether it sells or not. Referral and fulfillment fees are tied to the sale — no sale, no charge for those two. That split is why slow-moving inventory quietly costs money even before you discount it.

Individual plan or Professional plan — which is cheaper?

At $0.99 per item versus $39.99 flat per month, the math crosses over around 40 units a month — below that, Individual can cost less on fees alone; above it, Professional's flat fee wins. Professional also unlocks bulk tools, advertising and Brand Registry eligibility, which matters more than the few dollars once you're selling seriously.

Why did my FBA fulfillment fee change without me changing my price?

Because the fulfillment fee is driven by size and weight tier, not price. A packaging change, a supplier switch, or Amazon re-measuring a returned unit can shift the tier and change the fee, with no notification pointing at the cause.

Can I dispute an incorrect FBA fee?

Yes. If a fee was charged against the wrong size tier, category, or on a unit that was lost or damaged in an Amazon warehouse, that's a reimbursement case with a defined filing process and window. It won't be applied automatically — someone has to catch the discrepancy and file it.
