These are different categories, not rival tiers. Cin7 is multichannel inventory and order management with three published monthly prices. NetSuite is a full ERP with a general ledger, multi-entity consolidation and a quoted price. If you need consolidated statutory accounts, only one of them qualifies.

The comparison most pages get backwards

Search results for this query mostly line the two up feature by feature, which produces a grid where NetSuite wins every row and tells you nothing. The grid is wrong because it assumes the two products are competing for the same job. They are not.

Cin7 Core is a system of record for stock and orders. It holds SKUs, stock across locations, purchase orders, suppliers, landed cost, B2B and wholesale orders, and it syncs the result into your accounting package. Your accounting package remains your ledger.

NetSuite is the ledger, and everything attached to it. General ledger, AP and AR, consolidation across subsidiaries, multi-currency accounting, statutory reporting, with inventory and order management as modules that sit on top of it.

So the question is not "which is better". It is "do I need my inventory system and my ledger to be the same system?" — and for a great many Amazon-led brands the answer is genuinely no. Keep your accounting where it is, put a capable inventory system next to it, and you have solved the problem for a fraction of the money and a fraction of the project.

The answer flips the moment you have more than one legal entity. Consolidation, intercompany eliminations and multi-jurisdiction reporting are not features you bolt onto an inventory system; they are the reason the ERP category exists.

Price: one publishes, one quotes

Read on 21 August 2026, in US dollars, excluding tax, billed monthly with no annual toggle shown on the page:

  • Cin7 Core Standard — $349 a month. 5 users, 2 e-commerce and app integrations, 6,000 sale orders a year, unlimited inventory locations.
  • Cin7 Core Pro — $599 a month. 10 users, 4 integrations, 24,000 sale orders a year, material requirements planning.
  • Cin7 Core Advanced — $1,199 a month. 15 users, 6 integrations, 120,000 sale orders a year.
  • Cin7 Omni — the enterprise product, contact for pricing.

NetSuite publishes nothing. There is no pricing link in its main site navigation, and the URL that ranked on pricing queries for years now returns a 404. The quote is assembled from three components — the platform edition, the modules you switch on, and named-user licences — plus a separately quoted one-time implementation fee. Stated neutrally: they quote on a call, which is entirely normal for enterprise ERP, because there is no single honest number to publish.

The consequence for a buyer is a scoping difference more than a cost difference. With Cin7 you can budget on the pricing page and be roughly right. With NetSuite you cannot budget at all until scope is defined, and the implementation is frequently the larger number in year one. Neither approach is wrong; they suit different purchase processes.

One thing to watch on the Cin7 side that the headline does not show: the annual sale-order allowance is the real meter. Going from 24,000 to 24,001 sale orders in a year moves you from $599 to $1,199 a month — one order roughly doubling the bill. Pick on next year's order count, not this year's, and get in writing what happens if you exceed the allowance mid-term.

We should also concede our own position. Dr. Stock publishes no price. Cin7 publishes three. On transparency Cin7 is ahead of us and of NetSuite both.

Where Cin7 stops, stated honestly

If you are leaning toward the cheaper option, these are the things you will not get, and each one is a reason people end up buying an ERP eventually.

  • A general ledger. Cin7 syncs to your accounting package; it is not your accounting package. Two systems means a reconciliation relationship to maintain.
  • Multi-entity consolidation. If you have subsidiaries with intercompany transactions to eliminate, this is the wall.
  • Multi-currency accounting as distinct from multi-currency selling — translation, revaluation, statutory reporting in more than one jurisdiction.
  • Revenue recognition and subscription billing on the same ledger as product sales.
  • Deep manufacturing. Cin7 Core includes material requirements planning from the Pro tier, which covers assembly and light manufacture well. Full production costing, work orders and multi-level BOM depth is ERP territory.

And where Cin7 is genuinely strong, which anyone using it would confirm: unlimited inventory locations on every tier, a straightforward multichannel stock picture, purchase orders and supplier management that a small operations team can actually run, and — not a small thing — a published price you can put in a budget without a sales call.

Where NetSuite is oversized, stated just as honestly

The opposite failure is more expensive and more common than the first one, because it is harder to reverse.

NetSuite is oversized when you have one legal entity, one currency, and one or two sales channels. In that shape you are buying consolidation machinery for a business with nothing to consolidate, and paying an implementation to configure it. The recurring cost is the smaller half of that mistake; the larger half is the months of internal attention the project consumes.

It is also oversized when nobody internal will own it. An ERP without a controller or finance lead who owns the configuration becomes an expensive database that people work around. This is the single most reliable predictor of a disappointing ERP project, and it has nothing to do with the software.

Two things temper that, and they belong here rather than in a footnote. NetSuite's public record on the large B2B platforms is strong: 4.1 out of 5 from 4,919 reviews on G2 and 4.2 from 2,062 on Capterra, both read 21 August 2026. The praise is consistent and specific — finance, inventory, purchasing and sales in one record, with drill-down from a report straight to the transaction. And the honest reading of the complaints, which sit inside the four- and five-star reviews rather than the one-star ones, is that reporting takes real expertise to build. That is a configuration cost, not a defect, and it is the argument for budgeting the implementation properly rather than for avoiding the product.

For Cin7, the public records we could verify are Capterra at 4.3 from 738 reviews for Cin7 Core and 4.3 from 601 for Cin7 Omni, plus a Trustpilot TrustScore of 4.4 from 441 reviews, all read 21 August 2026. G2 carries a Cin7 Core listing with 346 reviews, but the score our read returned was inconsistent, so we are not printing it. That is the rule we work to: if we cannot stand behind a figure, the argument gets made without it rather than borrowing one from somewhere else.

The three tests that actually decide this

Skip the feature grid. Answer these three and the choice makes itself.

Test one — how many sets of statutory accounts do you file? If the answer is more than one, or will be within eighteen months, you are buying an ERP and the rest of this page is context. If it is one, keep reading.

Test two — who is going to own the system on a Tuesday? Name the person. If that person is the founder, and the founder is also running the business, an ERP implementation is a second job you have just created. An inventory system with a published price and an operations owner is a far more survivable project.

Test three — what is the actual pain, in numbers? Write down the three things going wrong and put a figure next to each. "Stock number disagrees across channels" is a Cin7-shaped problem. "Month-end takes eleven days and finance rebuilds it in a spreadsheet" is a NetSuite-shaped problem. "We are paying more in Amazon fees than we can explain" is neither, and buying either one will not fix it.

One prerequisite sits under all three. Whichever you pick, somebody has to establish landed cost per SKU before the data moves, because both systems inherit blank cost fields rather than repairing them. Name that person and the date, in writing, before you sign. Any proposal that promises margin improvement without naming the data it needs from you is promising something it cannot deliver — a fair test to apply to us as much as to either vendor here.

Neither of them moves your stock faster

Here is the boundary both products share, and it is where a surprising amount of money hides on an Amazon-led business.

A system of record tells you where stock is. It does not change how quickly stock becomes sellable. On seasonal accounts we use full-truckload freight with a live unload, which can take inventory from warehouse to sellable at Amazon in about a day, against a typical check-in queue of roughly two weeks. That is a practice we run across seasonal clients rather than a universal guarantee — but for a product with a ten-week selling window, that difference is most of the season, and it changes the cash cycle as well, because proceeds arrive days rather than weeks after the truck leaves. It is one of the few operational levers measured in weeks of selling rather than percentage points of fee, which is exactly why it never appears in software comparisons.

The same seam holds the rest of it: fulfilment fee bands set from Amazon's recorded dimensions rather than yours, aged-inventory surcharges accruing per unit against a calendar, shipment discrepancies inside short claim windows, and cash trapped in SKUs that stopped moving. Neither Cin7 nor NetSuite is built to work any of those, and neither claims to be.

Which one you should actually pick

Count your legal entities first. More than one, or one soon, and NetSuite is the category you are buying — budget the implementation as the larger year-one line and name the finance owner before you sign. One entity with a messy multichannel stock picture, and Cin7 Core is the cheaper, faster, published-price answer. If the pain is Amazon fees, stockouts and dead stock, neither system fixes it — that is Dr. Stock, from Full Circle.

What to do with this

Neither of these files a fee dispute for you. Before you pick, run one check: take your ten highest-volume ASINs, compare Amazon's recorded package dimensions against your own measured dimensions, and flag anything where the size tier looks wrong. Then ask each vendor what happens next — who measures, who files, who follows up.

Common questions

Is Cin7 a replacement for NetSuite?

Only if what you needed from NetSuite was inventory and order management. Cin7 Core is a system of record for stock and orders and syncs into your accounting package; it is not a general ledger and it does not consolidate subsidiaries. If your requirement includes multi-entity consolidation, multi-currency accounting, statutory reporting in more than one jurisdiction or revenue recognition, Cin7 is not an alternative and no honest comparison would say it is.

Which is cheaper, Cin7 or NetSuite?

Cin7 is, materially, but the comparison is only fair where the two overlap. Cin7 Core publishes $349, $599 and $1,199 a month billed monthly, read 21 August 2026. NetSuite publishes nothing and quotes on a call, with the implementation billed separately and frequently exceeding the first year's subscription. If you only need inventory and order management, you are paying ERP money for machinery you will not use.

When should an Amazon brand move from Cin7 to NetSuite?

When you file more than one set of statutory accounts, or will within eighteen months. Secondary triggers: intercompany transactions to eliminate, multi-currency accounting rather than multi-currency selling, revenue recognition obligations, or auditors asking for a single defensible system of record. A third condition matters just as much — you need a controller or finance lead who will own the configuration, or the project underdelivers regardless of the software.

Does Cin7 handle Amazon FBA fees?

No, and neither does NetSuite. Both record what Amazon charged; neither has a view on whether the charge was correct. Fulfilment fee bands are set from Amazon's own recorded package dimensions rather than the ones in your system, aged-inventory surcharges accrue per unit against a date, and shipment discrepancies sit inside short claim windows. That work is done outside any system of record, which is where Dr. Stock operates.
