What Jungle Scout publishes today, and where the prices moved to

First, a navigation note that matters if you are comparing quotes, because it changed. The main junglescout.com/pricing page no longer prints tier prices at all. Read on 21 August 2026, it splits the audience by revenue — sellers under $1M in annual revenue go to Catalyst, businesses above $1M go to Cobalt — and sends you on. The figures live one click deeper, at /pricing/catalyst-plans/. Cobalt has no published price and routes to a demo.

That matters because a great many articles quoting "Jungle Scout pricing" are quoting a page that has been restructured underneath them. Go to the plans page, not the pricing page, and note which of the two billing tabs is selected when you read it.

Two naming facts worth having, since both cause confusion in comparisons. The seller product is now branded Catalyst. And Downstream was absorbed into Jungle Scout Cobalt — every "Downstream pricing" figure still circulating prices a product that no longer exists on its own.

Both billing tabs, side by side — and a correction we owe you

Here are all six figures, from junglescout.com/pricing/catalyst-plans/, read 21 August 2026, in US dollars.

  • Starter — $29 a month on annual billing, invoiced as $348 a year. $49 a month billed monthly.
  • Growth Accelerator — $49 a month on annual billing, invoiced as $588 a year. $79 a month billed monthly.
  • Brand Owner + CI — $129 a month on annual billing, invoiced as $1,548 a year. $149 a month billed monthly.

The page advertises "save up to 40% on annual plans" and prints a per-tier saving of $240, $360 and $240. Now run the standing check — take the annual total, divide by twelve, and see whether it equals the monthly figure:

  • Starter: $348 ÷ 12 = $29.00. Twelve monthly payments would be $588. The saving is $240, or 40.8%. The page says $240.
  • Growth Accelerator: $588 ÷ 12 = $49.00. Twelve monthly payments would be $948. The saving is $360, or 38.0%. The page says $360.
  • Brand Owner: $1,548 ÷ 12 = $129.00. Twelve monthly payments would be $1,788. The saving is $240, or 13.4%. The page says $240.

All three reconcile exactly. The advertised discount is real, and "up to 40%" is precisely accurate — 40.8% on the entry tier is the maximum, and the top tier saves 13.4%. That last number is the one worth carrying away: the annual commitment is a much better deal at the bottom of the ladder than at the top.

Now the correction, because we got this wrong once and the correction is more useful than the original claim. An earlier version of our own analysis stated that Jungle Scout's annual figures were exactly twelve times the monthly, and invited readers to doubt the advertised discount. That was false. We had compared the annual rate expressed per month against the annual total — two different things — and concluded a discount was illusory when it is real and it is 40.8% at the entry tier. The page carrying that claim was unpublished rather than patched.

The rule that came out of it is worth more than the gotcha we thought we had: check which billing tab you are reading, on any vendor including us — and if a vendor's numbers look dishonest, assume first that you have misread the page. Killing your own gotcha is worth more than landing it.

The limits are the actual product, and they decide your tier

Nobody buys a price. They buy the allowances behind it, and Jungle Scout's ladder is steeper on allowances than on money. From the plan comparison table, same read:

  • Product Tracker — 50 products on Starter, 150 on Growth Accelerator, 2,000 on Brand Owner.
  • Historical product tracking — 30 days, then 6 months, then 2 years.
  • Sales Estimator — 500 estimates a month, then 1,000, then 5,000.
  • Keyword Scout — 50 searches a month, then 250, then unrestricted.
  • Historical keyword data — 1 month, then 1 year, then 2 years.
  • Opportunity Finder and Product Database — capped at 100 searches a month on Starter, unrestricted above it.
  • Data export, search trends and the supplier database — not on Starter at all.

Two consequences. First, Starter is a trial-shaped plan rather than a working one if you are doing real research: no export, one month of keyword history, and fifty keyword searches will not survive a serious product hunt. Second, the jump that actually matters is Growth Accelerator to Brand Owner, because it is where two years of history and the competitive-intelligence features arrive — and where the annual discount shrinks to 13.4%.

The honest way to pick is to work backwards from history depth. If your decisions rest on seasonality — and in most categories they do — one month of keyword history cannot show you a season and one year shows you one. That is a research-quality question, not a budget question.

Seats and segments — the two lines that are not in the headline

Two published charges sit outside the tier price, and both are easy to miss.

Seats. Starter includes one user and is not eligible for additional seats at all. Growth Accelerator includes one, Brand Owner includes three, and both can add more at $49 per seat per month, or $459 per seat per year. Run the division again: $459 ÷ 12 = $38.25, against $49 billed monthly — so the annual seat saves $129 a year, about 22%. It is a different basis, correctly labelled on the page.

The practical trap is the first one. A two-person business on Starter cannot add a second login, so the second person shares credentials or the business upgrades. That is a real cost step that never appears next to the headline price.

Segments, on Brand Owner. Five are included; additional segments are $99 a month per five segments, or $297 per three months per five segments. Divide the quarterly figure by three and you get $99 — the same monthly rate. So the quarterly option is a commitment rather than a discount. That is not a criticism and the page does not claim otherwise; it is simply worth knowing before you assume the longer commitment saves money, because on the plan tiers immediately above it, it does.

Everything on the plans page comes with a 7-day money-back guarantee and cancel-any-time, which materially lowers the risk of getting the tier wrong. Take the trial on the tier you think you need rather than the one below it — you learn more from hitting the real limits than from hitting artificial ones.

Cobalt, the part with no published price

Above $1M in annual revenue, Jungle Scout points you at Cobalt, and there is no figure on the site. It routes to a demo. Stated neutrally: they quote on a call, and the pricing FAQ says Cobalt charges per user.

What the comparison table does publish is the capability gap, and it is wide. Cobalt covers 19 Amazon marketplaces against Catalyst's 8, tracks up to 20,000 ASINs against 200, exposes over 800 metrics against up to 140, allows 6 platform integrations against 1, and adds an advertising layer Catalyst does not have at all — PPC management, bid optimisation, dayparting, rules automation, budget control and DSP management and reporting — plus 1P versus 3P sales analysis, buy box and competitive offers, and digital shelf analytics.

So the two products are not tiers of one thing. Catalyst is research and launch. Cobalt is market intelligence with media buying attached. If somebody has quoted you "Jungle Scout" without saying which, that is the first question to ask.

The buyer's asks for any quote-only product are the same every time: get the structure before the number — per user, per marketplace, per ASIN band, or a flat platform fee — plus the renewal uplift, the notice period, and written notice of fee changes. Ask those of any vendor including us. We publish no price for Dr. Stock either, and Jungle Scout publishing Catalyst's tiers openly while we publish nothing is a point in their favour.

What a research subscription cannot do, and when to stop paying for one

Here is the concession that matters more than any tier comparison. Research tools are bought to answer a question, and questions get answered. If you have already chosen your catalogue, sourced it and launched it, a product-research subscription becomes the easiest line on your software bill to cancel — and a great many sellers keep paying for it out of habit long after the last decision it informed. That is not a criticism of Jungle Scout, whose ratings above are strong; it is a criticism of how the category gets bought.

The test is simple: name the decision you will make in the next ninety days using this data. If you cannot, you are paying for reassurance.

There is also a whole class of money that no research tool touches, because it sits after the buy rather than before it. One of our own standing rules illustrates the seam. Sponsored ads start the day inventory is receivable at Amazon — never before. That is a practice we hold across managed accounts, and it sounds trivial until you see how often a launch calendar is set against a shipment date rather than a check-in date. Those two dates are routinely a fortnight apart and, in a bad season, further. Spend before stock is live buys clicks to a page that cannot convert, and the early conversion data is what the campaign's later performance is built on. A research tool will tell you the product is a good idea. It will not tell you the launch date is wrong.

Which one you should actually pick

Jungle Scout Catalyst is a well-rated research suite with genuinely transparent pricing, and its annual discount checks out tier by tier. Buy Growth Accelerator or above if you are doing real research; Starter cannot export or add a seat. Cobalt is a different product for $1M+ brands and is quote-only. Once the catalogue decisions are made, cancel it. The money after the buy is fees, stockouts and dead stock — that is Dr. Stock, from Full Circle.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, price the leak. Open your FBA storage fee and aged-inventory surcharge lines for the last twelve months, add the units you were out of stock on your best sellers, and add the value of every SKU that has not moved in 180 days. That total is the number the purchase has to move. A cheaper seat that nobody has time to drive will not move it.

How much does Jungle Scout cost per month?

Read on junglescout.com/pricing/catalyst-plans/ on 21 August 2026, in US dollars: on annual billing, Catalyst Starter is $29 a month, Growth Accelerator $49 and Brand Owner + CI $129, invoiced as $348, $588 and $1,548 a year. Is the Jungle Scout annual discount real?

Yes, and we checked it tier by tier because we once got this wrong. Divide each annual total by twelve and it matches the advertised annual rate exactly, so the two columns are genuinely different billing bases. Against twelve monthly payments the annual plan saves $240 on Starter (40.8%), $360 on Growth Accelerator (38.0%) and $240 on Brand Owner (13.4%). Which Jungle Scout plan do I actually need?

Pick on history depth and export, not on price. Starter has no data export, no supplier database, one month of keyword history and 50 keyword searches a month, which makes it a trial-shaped plan rather than a working one for real research. Growth Accelerator adds export and a year of history. Brand Owner is the tier that carries two years of history plus the competitive-intelligence features, and it is where the annual discount narrows.

Does Jungle Scout charge extra for users?

Yes, and the entry tier cannot add them at all. Starter includes one seat and is not eligible for additional seats. Growth Accelerator includes one and Brand Owner three, with extra seats at $49 per month or $459 per year each — the annual seat working out at $38.25 a month, saving $129 a year. On Brand Owner, additional segments are $99 a month per five, or $297 per three months, which is the same monthly rate.
