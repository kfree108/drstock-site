The published plans, read from Jungle Scout's own page

Jungle Scout splits into two products. Catalyst is the self-serve tool aimed at smaller sellers. Cobalt is the enterprise brand-analytics product for larger brands, and it carries no published price — it is a demo and a per-user quote, which is normal at that end of the market and worth stating neutrally rather than treating as a red flag.

These are the Catalyst figures showing on their pricing page on 20 August 2026, in US dollars:

  • Jungle Scout Starter — $29 a month, or $348 billed annually. One seat. 50 product tracker slots, 500 sales estimates a month, 100 opportunity finder searches, 50 keyword searches.
  • Jungle Scout Growth Accelerator — $49 a month, or $588 billed annually. One seat; extra seats, $49 a month each. 150 tracker slots, 1,000 sales estimates a month, unlimited opportunity finder, 250 keyword searches.
  • Jungle Scout Brand Owner with CI — $129 a month, or $1,548 billed annually. Three seats; extra seats, $49 a month each. 2,000 tracker slots, 5,000 sales estimates a month, unlimited opportunity finder and keyword searches.

There is a seven-day money-back guarantee on Catalyst. Prices in this category move — Jungle Scout's page has returned different figures on different reads before now — so treat all of the above as a snapshot and check the live page before you commit.

Read the billing toggle before you commit twelve months

Here is a small thing worth doing yourself, on any vendor, including us. When we read the Catalyst page the annual figures were exactly twelve times the monthly figures: $29 × 12 = $348, $49 × 12 = $588, $129 × 12 = $1,548. The page also carries a save-up-to-40% message.

There is nothing sinister in that. Promotional framing, regional pricing, a discount applied later in checkout, or a straightforward A/B test could all explain it, and the figures may well read differently by the time you visit. We are not going to characterise a vendor's intent from one page read, and neither should any page you find on this keyword.

What we are saying is that the check takes ten seconds and the commitment is a year long. Multiply the monthly figure by twelve and compare it to the annual figure yourself, on the day you buy, before you click. If the annual price is genuinely lower, take the discount. If it is the same, take the flexibility instead — a monthly plan you can leave is worth something real when your business changes shape in month four.

What actually decides your tier — and it is not your revenue

The plan names are aimed at seller stages, but the thing that will actually force you up a tier is usage limits: sales estimates per month, keyword searches, product tracker slots, and seats. That is worth internalising, because it tells you what Jungle Scout is priced to sell.

You are buying discovery capacity. How many products you can evaluate, how many keywords you can pull, how many ideas you can put through the funnel this month. That is an enormously valuable thing to buy when your bottleneck is what should we sell next. It is a far less valuable thing to buy when your bottleneck is why did this quarter's margin disappear on products we already sell.

Most brands cross that line without noticing, and keep renewing the research subscription for two years after research stopped being the constraint. Before you renew, ask yourself honestly: in the last ninety days, how many decisions did this tool actually cause? If the answer is fewer than five, you are on the wrong tier — possibly the wrong product.

Jungle Scout's Inventory Manager: what it is, honestly

Jungle Scout includes an Inventory Manager, and it is a decent piece of software. It syncs with Seller Central, uses your sales velocity, historical trends and seasonality to forecast demand, and flags each SKU as Reorder Now, Reorder Soon, Overstock or In Stock, with a suggested order date and quantity, supplier lead times and estimated costs.

If you have never had a restock calendar, that is a real upgrade over a spreadsheet and it will stop the obvious stockouts. Credit where it is due.

What it does not do is the part that costs money after the forecast. It does not place the purchase order, negotiate the lead time when your supplier slips, decide whether the 200 units aging past 181 days should be removed, liquidated or repriced, check whether Amazon has your product in the wrong size tier, reconcile an inbound shipment that arrived short, or file the reimbursement claim inside Amazon's window. A flag that says Overstock is a description of a problem. Somebody still has to be the person who does something about it on a Tuesday.

Where Jungle Scout is the right buy, and where the money moves elsewhere

Buy Jungle Scout if you are researching or launching. At $29 or $49 a month it is one of the cheapest ways to avoid a five-figure mistake on a product that was never going to work. The interface is cleaner than most of the category, the estimates are well regarded, and for a first or second product it is very hard to argue against. If you are a seven-figure brand wanting share-of-category and competitor tracking, Cobalt is a serious product and worth the demo.

Look elsewhere when your constraint is working capital. This is the argument that gets skipped on every page ranking for this keyword. For an established catalogue, the largest recoverable sums are usually not in finding a better product. They are in the cash you already own and cannot use: units sitting past 181 days accruing an aged-inventory surcharge, a storage utilisation surcharge on stock that outgrew its sales velocity, hero SKUs that went out of stock and lost rank, ASINs paying the wrong fulfilment fee because Amazon's recorded dimensions do not match the product, and inbound shipments that reconciled short and were never claimed.

Five things to settle before you subscribe

  • Which limit will you hit first? Sales estimates, keyword searches or seats. Price the tier you will be on in month three, not month one.
  • How many seats do you really need? At $49 a month each, three extra seats cost more than the plan. Decide who genuinely logs in.
  • Monthly × 12 versus the annual price, checked on the day you buy.
  • What is the decision this causes? Name it before you subscribe. If you cannot, the subscription is a hobby.
  • Is research actually your constraint? Total your last twelve months of storage fees and aged-inventory surcharges first. If that number is larger than your annual software budget, you are shopping in the wrong aisle.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, price the leak. Open your FBA storage fee and aged-inventory surcharge lines for the last twelve months, add the units you were out of stock on your best sellers, and add the value of every SKU that has not moved in 180 days. That total is the number the purchase has to move. A cheaper seat that nobody has time to drive will not move it.
