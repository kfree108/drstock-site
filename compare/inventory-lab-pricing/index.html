The standalone price no longer exists

Type the obvious pricing URL into a browser and you do not land on a pricing page. You are forwarded to Threecolts, where the product is presented as part of Seller 365 and InventoryLab is included in every plan, badged as ten apps for the price of one.

That redirect makes most of what is written about this product's cost out of date. Many articles, comparison tables and AI answers still quote a standalone monthly subscription. Those figures were accurate once. They now price something you cannot buy.

This is not a criticism of anybody. Consolidation is a common pattern in Amazon software: strong point tools get folded into a suite and lose their individual price page. Search results simply decay more slowly than product catalogues.

The practical rule: before you budget from any figure for an Amazon tool, open the vendor's own URL and see where it lands. If it forwards to a different company, the number you were about to use describes history.

What the bundle costs now, and which figures we will print

Read from the Threecolts pricing page on 20 August 2026, in US dollars, on the billed-monthly view:

  • Standard: $69 a month. One user.
  • Teams: $129 a month. Up to ten users.
  • Pro: $199 a month. Up to ten users.

All three carry access to the ten tools and a 14-day free trial, extended in two halves: seven days on signup and seven more once a seller account is connected.

An annual option exists too, and we are deliberately not printing it. The two Threecolts pages we opened presented annual figures differently from one another, and we would rather send you to the live toggle than pick the version that suits the story. Pricing pages often hide a second set of numbers behind a switch. Check which billing tab you are reading, on any vendor including us. Prices change, so confirm on the vendor's page before you buy.

The ten tools cover sourcing and scanning, listing and shipment building, repricing, buyer messaging and feedback alerts, profit analytics and accounting. If you already pay separately for two or three of those, the bundle arithmetic gets interesting quickly.

The line most comparisons miss: the reimbursement commission

In the plan detail rather than the headline is a second price, and for some sellers it is larger than the subscription. Each Seller 365 tier carries a reimbursement commission: 15% on Standard and Teams, and 5% on Pro, per the Threecolts plan detail we read on 20 August 2026.

That deserves credit. GETIDA, a well-known FBA recovery specialist, publishes performance-based pricing starting at 25% on its own pricing page, with no subscription attached. A 5% commission inside a $199 subscription is aggressive pricing, and it is published openly.

Break-even sum for Standard versus Pro. The subscription gap is $199 minus $69 = $130 a month, or $1,560 a year. The commission gap is 15% minus 5% = 10 points of whatever you recover. So $1,560 divided by 10% = $15,600 of recovered reimbursements a year is the break-even point.

  • For example, if you recover $20,000 a year, commission is $3,000 on Standard and $1,000 on Pro. Pro saves $2,000 in commission for a $1,560 upgrade, so it comes out $440 ahead.
  • For example, if you recover $8,000 a year, commission is $1,200 on Standard and $400 on Pro. That saves $800 against a $1,560 upgrade, so Standard is cheaper by $760.

Run this on your own reimbursement history before choosing a tier. Above the break-even, the expensive plan is the cheap one. Below it, it is not.

What InventoryLab is excellent at, and who should just buy it

We do not sell a sourcing workflow, so take this straight: for the seller it was built for, this is a very good product.

Its strength is the path from a scanned item to a live listing. You research a buy, calculate the margin with Amazon's fees taken off before you commit, build the shipment through a guided flow, and end up with cost of goods, expenses and profit tracked against the same records instead of rebuilt in a spreadsheet. Few tools handle that whole chain on one set of data.

Buy it, and stop comparison shopping, if any of these are you:

  • You source physical inventory (arbitrage, wholesale, liquidation) and the buying decision is the business.
  • You want fee-aware margin at the moment of purchase, not at month end.
  • Your bookkeeper needs a defensible cost trail per batch.
  • You already pay for two or three of the other tools in the bundle.

If profit reporting is all you want, compare against a dedicated analytics product. Sellerboard lists its entry plan at $19 a month billed monthly and is built for profit reconciliation. If you will not use the sourcing workflow, you would be paying for nine tools to get one.

A checklist before you pick any inventory tool

  • Open the vendor's own URL. Note where it lands and the date you read it.
  • Name the billing basis of every price: monthly, or annual shown per month.
  • Find the second price. Look for commissions, per-user charges and order or SKU bands.
  • Pull your own last 12 months of reimbursements and run the break-even sum above.
  • Ask who files the claim. A tool that identifies a candidate is different from one that opens the case and chases it.
  • Check what you already pay for. Overlap with repricing, accounting or profit tools changes the sum.

Where the software stops, and the work starts

Every product on this page ends at the same wall, and no pricing page prints it. The subscription buys visibility. Visibility generates a queue of work.

Stock does not fulfil orders, is not a system of record and is not an ERP. It is that queue run as a product. Consider what sits in it after the dashboard has done its job:

  • An identified reimbursement candidate is not a recovered dollar. Someone has to open the case, attach the evidence and chase it inside a limited window.
  • Amazon's FBA page says items held in a fulfilment centre beyond 181 days attract an aged-inventory surcharge on top of ordinary storage. Knowing a SKU is at day 170 is not a decision. Removing, liquidating, discounting or accepting the charge is.
  • A fulfilment fee that looks too high for the box is not a refund. Amazon bills against the dimensions it has recorded, and correcting them is a dispute somebody has to raise and follow through.
  • A restock alert is not a purchase order placed against a lead time with cash that is sitting elsewhere.

Amazon also changed the economics of one of those lines. According to Amazon's updated FBA inventory reimbursement policy, effective 31 March 2025, reimbursement valuation moved towards the manufacturing or sourcing cost of the item rather than its selling price. Accurate per-batch cost records, which is what the product on this page is good at, matter more than they used to.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, price the leak. Open your FBA storage fee and aged-inventory surcharge lines for the last twelve months, add the units you were out of stock on your best sellers, and add the value of every SKU that has not moved in 180 days. That total is the number the purchase has to move. A cheaper seat that nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does Inventory Lab cost now?

It is no longer priced on its own. It is included in every Seller 365 plan from Threecolts, which listed Standard at $69 a month, Teams at $129 and Pro at $199 on the billed-monthly view when we read it on 20 August 2026, all with a 14-day trial. An annual option exists behind the billing toggle. Standalone figures in older articles price a subscription that is not for sale.

Is the Seller 365 Pro plan worth the extra money?

Work it out from your recovery history. The commission is 15% on Standard and Teams and 5% on Pro, and the gap between Standard and Pro is $130 a month, or $1,560 a year. Divide $1,560 by the 10-point commission gap and the break-even is $15,600 of recovered reimbursements a year. Above that, Pro costs less overall; below it, Standard does.

What happened to Inventory Lab? Was it acquired?

It now sits inside the Seller 365 bundle alongside nine other tools, and its own pricing URL forwards to that bundle's page on Threecolts. The consequence for buyers is that older pricing articles keep describing a standalone product. Check where a vendor's own URL lands before you trust any figure attached to it.

Is Sellerboard cheaper than Inventory Lab?

On the sticker, yes: Sellerboard's entry tier is listed at $19 a month billed monthly against $69 for the bundle. They are not the same purchase. Sellerboard is dedicated profit and cost reconciliation. The bundle adds a sourcing-to-shipment workflow, nine other tools and a recovery commission rate. If you never scan a product or build a shipment, you are paying for capability you will not open.

Does any of this software file my FBA claims for me?

The bundle attaches a commission rate to recovery, which signals a filing service is part of what that percentage buys. Pure analytics products generally identify candidates and leave the filing to you. Identification is a database query. Filing is a person attaching evidence and chasing a case inside a limited window. Check which one you are buying.
