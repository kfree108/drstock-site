The short version, by what is actually blocking you

Feature-count comparisons are how this question usually gets answered, and they are close to useless because both suites have more features than you will use. The better frame is: what is stopping you this quarter?

  • "I do not know what to sell." Jungle Scout. Its entry tiers, $49 or $79 a month on monthly billing ($29 or $49 billed annually), get you to a go or no-go faster, with less to learn.
  • "My listings are not ranking for the terms that matter." Helium 10. The keyword and reverse-ASIN tooling is deeper, and rank tracking is more granular.
  • "I sell on Walmart or TikTok Shop too." Helium 10. Its marketplace coverage is broader; Jungle Scout is more Amazon-centred. Check both vendors' current lists, because this changes.
  • "I want ads in the same login." Helium 10 Diamond bundles Helium 10 Ads. Read the fee mechanic first — see below.
  • "I am past a million in revenue and need category share and competitor tracking." Jungle Scout Cobalt, on a demo and a quote.
  • "I know exactly what I sell and my margin is disappearing anyway." Neither. Skip to the section on what neither of them does.

Two structural notes that matter more than the headline numbers. First, Helium 10 Diamond includes Helium 10 Ads with a 2% management fee on PPC spend, described in their documentation as applying to spend in profiles set to Managed; we could not confirm a minimum-spend threshold on their own pages. That is a legitimate pricing model — we charge a percentage on Dr. PPC ourselves — but it is a second, variable line on the bill and it should be in your comparison. Second, Jungle Scout charges $49 a month for each extra seat, or $459 a year on an annual plan, so a three-person team on Growth Accelerator costs more than the plan does.

Compare like with like: total twelve months at your real seat count and your real ad spend, not the sticker on the pricing page.

Where the numbers come from, and how much to trust them

Both products sell you estimates of things Amazon does not publish: monthly units, revenue, search volume. Neither has a pipe into Amazon's ledger. Both build models from marketplace signals, and Helium 10 additionally leans on Amazon Brand Analytics data where a seller has brand access, which is genuine first-party search behaviour rather than a model.

The practical consequence is the same for both: treat the numbers as directional, not accounting. They are excellent for ranking one opportunity above another and poor for building a financial model. If you have ever seen two tools give different revenue estimates for the same ASIN, that is not a scandal — it is two models disagreeing, which is what models do.

The single best test of either tool's estimates is your own data. Take three ASINs you own, where you know the true monthly units, and see which tool lands closer on your category. That test takes twenty minutes, costs nothing, and beats every comparison page on the internet including this one.

The honest strengths on each side

Jungle Scout is better at getting to a decision. Fewer tools, cleaner interface, a shorter learning curve, and a workflow that runs product idea to shortlist to go or no-go without you having to know which of eighteen tools to open. For a first or second product, that simplicity is a feature and not a compromise. Inventory Manager is a solid restock forecaster. Cobalt is a serious enterprise product.

Helium 10 is better at depth and breadth. More keyword surface area, stronger reverse-ASIN work, more granular rank tracking, more marketplaces, and a genuinely wide operations set — Inventory Management, Profits, Alerts, Refund Genie for identifying FBA reimbursement candidates, plus a separately sold managed refund service. If your team is going to live in a tool five days a week, the extra depth pays off.

Neither of these is a criticism of the other. A seller who has outgrown Jungle Scout and moved to Helium 10 made a sensible decision; so did a seller who found Helium 10 sprawling and moved the other way.

What neither of them does

Both are software you drive. That is not a flaw — it is the category — but it sets a hard ceiling, and the ceiling is where most established brands' money actually goes.

Run a two-week bake-off instead of reading more comparisons

Both vendors sell month-to-month, and Jungle Scout offers a seven-day money-back guarantee on Catalyst. So stop reading and test. Here is the protocol.

  • Week zero. Write down the five questions you actually need answered this quarter. Real ones — "should we launch a second colourway", "why is ASIN X losing rank" — not "is the interface nice".
  • Buy both, monthly. Entry tiers are enough for a test. Do not commit to a year for a bake-off.
  • Week one, Jungle Scout. Week two, Helium 10. Same five questions, same order, timed.
  • Score three things: how long each question took, whether the answer was usable, and how much you had to learn to get it.
  • Then check the estimates against your own ASINs, where you already know the truth.
  • Cancel one. Whichever caused more decisions, keep. If neither caused a decision, cancel both and go and total your last twelve months of storage fees and aged-inventory surcharges instead — that is where the answer is.

Which one you should actually pick

Jungle Scout suits sellers whose problem is what to sell — cheaper at entry, faster to learn, and Cobalt is strong for larger brands. Helium 10 suits teams that will live in a tool daily and need keyword depth, more marketplaces or bundled ads. If you already know what you sell and the margin is going to fees, stockouts and trapped cash, buy the work instead.

What to do with this

Neither of these files a fee dispute for you. Before you pick, run one check: take your ten highest-volume ASINs, compare Amazon's recorded package dimensions against your own measured dimensions, and flag anything where the size tier looks wrong. Then ask each vendor what happens next — who measures, who files, who follows up.

Common questions

Which is better for beginners, Jungle Scout or Helium 10?

Jungle Scout, in most cases. Fewer tools and a cleaner workflow mean you spend your first month making decisions rather than learning software, and its Starter tier, at $49 a month on monthly billing or $29 billed annually, is a low-risk way to find out whether a product idea is worth inventory money. Sellers who need keyword depth tend to move to Helium 10 later, and that is a perfectly sensible progression rather than a sign of a wrong first choice.

Which one is cheaper?

Jungle Scout at the entry level — Starter and Growth Accelerator are $49 and $79 a month on monthly billing, or $29 and $49 billed annually, against Helium 10 Platinum at $129 monthly or $99 on annual billing. USD, read 20 August 2026. But compare at your real usage: Jungle Scout charges $49 a month per extra seat ($459 a year on an annual plan), and Helium 10 Diamond adds a 2% management fee on ad spend run through managed profiles. A three-seat team advertising heavily can end up with the ranking reversed.

Are the sales estimates accurate?

Both are models, not ledgers, and neither has access to Amazon's actual unit data for other people's products. Use them to rank opportunities against each other rather than to build a financial plan. The one test worth doing is on your own ASINs, where you know the real numbers — whichever tool is closer on your category is the more accurate tool for you, regardless of what any comparison page claims.

Can I use both?

Plenty of teams do, usually Helium 10 for keyword and rank work and Jungle Scout for opportunity screening. Whether that is worth it depends on whether two subscriptions cause more decisions than one. Run the bake-off above for a fortnight before you make it permanent, and total the twelve-month cost at your real seat count before you decide.

Do either of them handle FBA fees or reimbursements?

Partially, and only on the identification side. Helium 10 has Refund Genie for surfacing reimbursement candidates and sells a managed refund service separately; both suites show fee data in their profit reporting. Neither measures your product against Amazon's recorded size tier, disputes a misclassification, decides removal versus liquidation on aging stock, or files and chases claims inside Amazon's windows.
