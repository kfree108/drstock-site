How to review a suite without reviewing the brochure

Helium 10 is not one product. It is roughly thirty products behind one login, and any review that grades it as a single thing is grading a marketing page. The question that matters is narrower and much more useful: which of those tools will you open in a normal week, and how good is each of those?

Run the exercise on yourself before you read another comparison. Write down the last five things you did in a seller tool. For most brands past their first year the list looks something like: checked where a keyword ranks, looked at a competitor's listing, pulled a profit figure, checked whether stock was going to run out, and looked at why a fee changed. Five jobs. A suite that is superb at two of them and merely present for the other three is a different purchase from a suite that is good at all five, and the price is identical either way.

The rest of this review is organised that way — what Helium 10 is genuinely excellent at, where it is present rather than excellent, and what sits entirely outside the category no matter which tier you buy.

For the record on price, since reviews that duck it are not much use: Helium 10's own pricing page on 20 August 2026 shows Platinum at $129 a month, or $99 a month on annual billing, and Diamond at $359 a month, or $279 on annual billing, in US dollars, with an Enterprise tier quoted from $1,499 on annual billing. Diamond also carries a 2% management fee on advertising spend run through Helium 10 Ads, which is a normal and reasonable way to price ad management — we charge a percentage of spend ourselves on the advertising side of our own business.

What it is genuinely excellent at

We sell nothing in product research, so this is not a grudging concession. In its core discipline Helium 10 is one of the two or three best tools available to an Amazon seller, and if that discipline is your bottleneck you should buy it and stop reading.

  • Reverse-ASIN and keyword mining. Taking a competitor's ASIN and getting back the search terms it actually ranks and converts on is the single highest-leverage research move on Amazon, and this is the tool set people switch to Helium 10 for.
  • Product discovery at volume. Filtering the catalogue down to candidates by demand, competition, review count and fee profile, quickly, with an interface built for people doing it all day.
  • Listing construction. Building copy against a keyword bank, checking index status, and catching the day a listing changes underneath you.
  • Rank tracking with history. Knowing where you sit on the terms that matter and what moved after a launch, a price change or a competitor's promotion.
  • Reach beyond Amazon. Coverage extending to other marketplaces, including Walmart and TikTok Shop, matters more each year and is a real advantage over single-channel tools.

There is also a free tier and a set of separately-priced add-ons, which makes it easy to try the core before committing. If your job this quarter is deciding what to sell and how to describe it, this is where your money should go, and nothing further down this page changes that.

The tool-count trap, and the audit that ends the argument

Tool count is the most persuasive and least informative number on any suite's homepage. Thirty tools is not thirty times the value of one tool; past a handful, additional modules mostly compete for the same scarce resource, which is the attention of the one person on your team who logs in.

Here is a test that settles the renewal question better than any review, ours included. For the next thirty days, keep a plain text file. Every time anyone on your team opens a tool inside the suite, write the tool name and one line on what it was for. Nothing else — no scoring, no meetings.

At the end of the month you will have one of three lists:

  • Four to six names appear repeatedly, all in research and listings. Buy the tier that covers those, ignore the rest of the catalogue, and stop feeling guilty about unused modules. This is the healthy outcome and it is the most common one.
  • The file is nearly empty. The subscription is a standing charge for optionality. Either assign the account to a named person with recurring calendar blocks, or drop a tier.
  • The entries are mostly "checked why the fee went up" and "looked at stock". You are using a research suite as an operations console. It will do that adequately and it is not what it is best at, and the money is probably better spent elsewhere — see the next two sections.

Thirty days of that file is worth more than every listicle on this keyword put together, because it is about you rather than about the software.

Where the suite informs and something still has to act

Helium 10 has real operational modules and it would be dishonest to wave them away. Its inventory tooling forecasts and prompts restocks. Its refund tool surfaces FBA discrepancies that may be recoverable. Its profit view reconciles what you actually earned. Its alerting tells you when a listing or a fee moves. These are competent tools that do what the page says.

What they share is a design assumption: somebody is present. Each one produces a finding and hands it to you. That is entirely appropriate for software — it is what software is — but it means the value of the module is conditional on attendance in a way the pricing is not. The subscription is charged in the weeks nobody logs in. The value is not.

The gap shows up most sharply on the recovery side. A list of reimbursable discrepancies is not money; it becomes money only when someone opens each case, attaches the evidence, and follows it up when it stalls — inside claim windows that Amazon measures in weeks. Notably, Helium 10 sells a managed refund service separately from the software, which is the clearest possible acknowledgement from the vendor themselves that finding and recovering are two different products.

The same is true one step earlier. A restock alert is not a purchase order. A storage report is not a removal order. An anomalous fee is not a remeasurement request. In each case the suite has done its job the moment it shows you the thing, and the money moves only if a human then does theirs.

What sits outside the category entirely

Now the part that no research suite is built for, and that no tier upgrade will reach. These are the Amazon-side economics, and they are where a lot of margin quietly goes:

  • Size-tier misclassification. Amazon holds recorded dimensions for every ASIN and prices fulfilment from them. When the record disagrees with the packed product, you overpay on every unit shipped, indefinitely, and nothing about the error is loud. Correcting it needs a measurement, a request and a follow-through.
  • The ageing clock. Amazon's aged-inventory surcharge applies to units held past 181 days and steepens in bands as stock gets older, assessed against a snapshot taken monthly. Every slow SKU therefore has a decision date, and dashboards do not have decision dates.
  • Stockouts and the fee on the other side. Running out costs rank, which is expensive to buy back through advertising. Running too lean triggers a low-inventory-level charge instead. The correct answer is a narrow band, and hitting it is planning work, not reporting work.
  • Inbound discrepancies. Shipments that reconcile short against what you sent are one of the most commonly abandoned categories of claim, because reconciling them is tedious and the window closes.
  • Cash locked in dead SKUs. The dollar sitting in a SKU that has not moved since spring is not neutral; it is the dollar you could not put into the SKU that sold out. That is a portfolio decision made against a deadline, not a chart.

None of the above is a criticism of Helium 10. It is a category boundary, and it holds for every research suite on the market.

Which one you should actually pick

Helium 10 is an excellent research and listing suite and a competent operations reporter. If discovery, keywords and listings are your bottleneck, buy it — few tools return more per dollar for that job. If your weekly questions are about fees, stock cover and recovery, a suite will describe the problem accurately and leave it to you. That is when buying the work makes more sense than buying another login.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you need done, not the feature list. Pull your last three inbound shipment reconciliation reports and count the units received against units shipped, then pull your storage fees and aged-inventory surcharges for the last twelve months. Ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes those gaps, or only shows them to you on a dashboard.

Common questions

Is Helium 10 worth it in 2026?

If you are actively researching products, mining keywords or rebuilding listings, yes — it is among the best purchases available for that work and the annual rate on the entry paid tier is reasonable for what you get. If you have a settled catalogue and your weekly questions are about fees, stock cover and reimbursements, you are paying for a research suite to do an operations job it was not designed for, and the money will go further elsewhere.

Is the Helium 10 free plan enough to start?

It is enough to test whether the interface suits how you think and to sample the core research tools, which is exactly what a free tier should do. It is not enough to run a launch on, because the limits bite precisely at the point where research gets serious. Treat it as a fitting room rather than as a plan, and give yourself a fortnight of real tasks in it before deciding on a tier.

How accurate are the sales estimates?

Directionally useful, individually unreliable — which is true of every estimator in the category, because none of them see Amazon's actual order data. They are built to rank opportunities against each other, and they do that well enough. Treat a single ASIN's estimate as a hypothesis rather than a figure, and never build a purchase order on one. Once you are selling, your own data replaces estimates entirely and their importance falls away.

Does Helium 10 manage inventory?

It forecasts and prompts. It will tell you a SKU is heading for a shortage, show you the fees you are paying, and flag discrepancies that may be reimbursable. What it does not do is place the order, argue the size tier, file the claim or clear the aged pallet — the module ends at the notification, by design. Whether that is sufficient depends entirely on whether someone on your team has recurring hours booked to act on notifications.

What does Dr. Stock do that Helium 10 does not?

It does the acting. Same subject matter in places — fees, stock, reimbursements — but the deliverable is a completed action rather than a report: the claim filed inside the window, the remeasure requested, the aged stock decided before the surcharge band, the reorder prepared and brought to a human for the purchase call.
