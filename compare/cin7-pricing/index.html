The published plans, and what each one actually includes

Cin7 sells two products. Cin7 Core is the cloud inventory and order management system with published pricing. Cin7 Omni is the enterprise tier, described as fully customisable, and it carries no published price — contact sales. Figures below were read from cin7.com on 20 August 2026, in US dollars, excluding tax.

  • Cin7 Core Standard — $349 a month. Five users, 6,000 orders a year, two e-commerce and app integrations.
  • Cin7 Core Pro — $599 a month. Ten users, 24,000 orders a year, four integrations.
  • Cin7 Core Advanced — $1,199 a month. Fifteen users, 120,000 orders a year, six integrations.
  • Cin7 Omni — quote only. Eight users included, flexible order volume, five integrations.

All plans include unlimited inventory locations and 24/7 support, and more users can be purchased on any tier. One caution worth stating: third-party pricing directories currently list at least one of these tiers at a different figure from the vendor's own page. That is normal — prices move and aggregators lag — and it is exactly why the only figure worth acting on is the one on Cin7's page on the day you buy.

Read the order bands, because they are the price

The user counts get the attention and the order bands decide the bill. Convert them to something you can feel:

  • Standard, 6,000 orders a year — 500 orders a month, about 16 a day.
  • Pro, 24,000 a year — 2,000 a month, about 65 a day.
  • Advanced, 120,000 a year — 10,000 a month, about 330 a day.

Sixteen orders a day is a small brand. Any Amazon seller doing meaningful volume clears Standard's band quickly, which means the Standard price is not the entry price for most people reading this. Pro is, and Advanced arrives sooner than the jump in order count suggests, because the gap between 24,000 and 120,000 covers a very wide range of business sizes.

Price the tier you will be on in twelve months, not the one you qualify for today. Then ask what happens if you exceed the band mid-term: does it upgrade automatically, is it billed as overage, is it enforced at all? Get the answer in writing. Volume-banded software is perfectly reasonable and widely used — the failure mode is not the model, it is budgeting from the wrong band.

The number that is not on the pricing page

Implementation. Cin7 offers onboarding in several formats — group sessions, one-to-one, and partner-led — and publishes no dollar amount for any of them on the pricing page. Third-party sources put implementation for a system of this type in the low thousands, but we are not going to print someone else's number as though it were Cin7's, because we did not read it on their page.

What we will say is that this line is real and you must ask for it before you sign, along with four others that behave the same way:

  • Data migration — historical orders, purchase orders, supplier records, cost history.
  • Implementation partner fees, if you use one. Frequently larger than the software in year one, and frequently the right call anyway.
  • Additional users beyond the tier allowance.
  • Contract term — annual or monthly, and what the exit looks like.

Year one total cost for a system like this is routinely a multiple of twelve months of subscription. That is not a criticism of Cin7 — it is true of every inventory and order management system, and it is the single most common budgeting mistake in this purchase. Ask for a year-one total, in writing, with those five lines itemised.

What Cin7 is for, and when you genuinely need it

Be clear about the category. Cin7 is a multichannel inventory and order management system — a system of record. It holds your SKUs, stock levels across locations, purchase orders, suppliers, costs, B2B and wholesale orders, and it syncs the result into your accounting. That is a different kind of product from a research suite, a 3PL, or a managed service, and it solves a problem those cannot.

You genuinely need something in this category when:

  • You sell across several channels — your own store, Amazon, other marketplaces, wholesale, retail — and need one authoritative stock number.
  • You hold stock in more than one place: your own warehouse, a 3PL, FBA, plus in-transit.
  • You raise purchase orders against suppliers with lead times, and need landed cost tracking.
  • You have B2B or EDI customers with compliance requirements.
  • Your accounting has stopped agreeing with your stock, and somebody is reconciling it by hand every month.

If several of those are true, buy a system. We do not replace one and would not pretend to. Dr. Stock is not a system of record, it does not hold your master SKU data, and it is not an ERP. Anyone who tells you a managed service removes the need for an inventory system in a genuinely multichannel business is selling you a problem for later.

When you might be buying a system you do not need yet

The opposite case is worth stating just as plainly, because it is common and expensive. If you sell only on Amazon, FBA does your fulfilment, you hold one pool of stock, and your purchase orders go to two or three suppliers — the system of record you already have is Seller Central plus your accounting package. Adding a mid-tier platform subscription with a year-one implementation bill on top will not, by itself, recover a dollar of margin. It will give you a tidier version of information you already had.

In that situation the money is somewhere else entirely, and it is usually in four places:

  • Fee misclassification. Amazon assigns a size tier from its recorded dimensions. When those are wrong, you pay the wrong fulfilment fee on every unit until someone measures the product and reconciles it.
  • Storage and aged inventory. Monthly storage fees, a storage utilisation surcharge when stock outruns velocity, and an aged-inventory surcharge whose tiers begin at 181 days — assessed against a monthly inventory snapshot, so the decision has a date attached.
  • Stockouts and their aftermath. Rank lost mid-campaign is expensive to buy back, and the low-inventory-level fee charges you from the other direction.
  • Unclaimed money. Inbound shipments that reconcile short, lost and damaged units, fee overcharges. Amazon auto-credits some categories now; others still need a case opened, and current policy describes most fulfilment-centre claims as filed within 60 days.

None of that is fixed by better bookkeeping. It is fixed by somebody doing the work, on a schedule.

Which one you should actually pick

Cin7 Core is a solid multichannel inventory and order management system, and if you have several channels, several stock locations and purchase orders to manage, it earns its price — budget for the Pro tier rather than the Standard headline, plus an implementation cost that is not published. If you sell only on Amazon and the margin is going to fees and stockouts, buy the work instead.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, price the leak. Open your FBA storage fee and aged-inventory surcharge lines for the last twelve months, add the units you were out of stock on your best sellers, and add the value of every SKU that has not moved in 180 days. That total is the number the purchase has to move. A cheaper seat that nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does Cin7 cost per month?

Cin7 Core Standard — $349 a month; Cin7 Core Pro — $599; Cin7 Core Advanced — $1,199, in US dollars excluding tax, as displayed on cin7.com on 20 August 2026. Cin7 Omni is quote-only. Check the live page before budgeting — at least one third-party directory currently lists a different figure for one of these tiers, which is a reminder that aggregators lag and vendors change prices.

Does Cin7 charge for implementation?

Onboarding is offered in group, one-to-one and partner-led formats, and no dollar figure for any of them appears on the pricing page. That is a finding rather than an accusation — most systems in this category price implementation on scope. Ask for a written year-one total that itemises implementation, data migration, any implementation-partner fees, additional users and the contract term. For a system of this type, year one commonly costs a multiple of twelve months of subscription.

What decides which Cin7 tier I need?

Annual order volume, more than headcount. The bands are 6,000, 24,000 and 120,000 orders a year, which is roughly 16, 65 and 330 orders a day. Most Amazon brands with real volume are past the Standard band, so price Pro or Advanced rather than the headline entry figure. Ask what happens if you exceed your band mid-term — automatic upgrade, overage billing, or nothing.

Is Cin7 worth it for an Amazon-only seller?

Often not yet. If FBA does your fulfilment, you hold one pool of stock and you buy from a handful of suppliers, Seller Central plus your accounting package already is your system of record, and a multichannel platform will reorganise information rather than recover money. The case changes the moment you add a second sales channel, a second stock location, wholesale customers, or manual reconciliation that somebody does by hand each month.
