The free tier hiding inside your own Seller Central

Every comparison page on this keyword starts by listing paid tools. That skips a step, and the skipped step is the one that saves you money.

If you have a Professional selling account and you are enrolled in Brand Registry, Amazon already hands you a set of first-party datasets that nobody else can match, because they come from Amazon's own orders rather than from a model:

  • Search Query Performance. For your own ASINs, the actual search terms shoppers used, with impressions, clicks, cart adds and purchases, plus your share of each. No estimator can approach this — it is ground truth about the exact terms that turn into money for you.
  • Top Search Terms. Ranked search frequency across the marketplace with the leading clicked ASINs behind each term. This is the backbone of demand research, and it is free.
  • Product Opportunity Explorer. Niches sized by search volume, click share, average price, seasonality and launch success rate. It is a genuinely strong discovery tool that a striking number of sellers have never opened.
  • Market Basket and Repeat Purchase analysis. What else your buyers purchase alongside your product, and how often they come back. Bundle and subscription decisions live here.
  • The FBA revenue calculator and your own fee preview report. Fee reality per ASIN, from the source that charges them.

Spend two evenings inside those before you spend anything at all. A fair number of brands discover that what they were paying a suite for was a friendlier interface over data they already owned — which is a legitimate thing to buy, but it should be a conscious purchase rather than an accidental one.

What is genuinely still worth paying for

Amazon's first-party reporting has real limits, and this is where third-party tools earn their subscription. Be precise about which of these you actually need, because each one points at a different product.

  • Competitor keyword mining. Amazon shows you your terms, not theirs. Taking somebody else's ASIN and getting back the terms it ranks and converts on is the single most valuable thing a paid suite does, and there is no free equivalent.
  • Historical price, rank and offer data. Seller Central is thin on history and shows you nothing about competitors' past pricing or buy box behaviour. Long-run history is a specialist product.
  • Brand and seller mapping. Who owns which brands, which sellers are on which listings, how a category is structured competitively. Amazon has no reason to show you this.
  • Cross-marketplace coverage. If you sell on more than one marketplace, first-party reporting fragments and third-party tooling consolidates.
  • Speed of iteration. Seller Central reports are functional and slow. A tool that turns a forty-minute task into four minutes is worth paying for if you do that task weekly.

Write down which of those five you need. That list, not a feature grid, is your shortlist criteria — and for a lot of settled brands the honest list has one item on it.

The paid shortlist, by what each is actually best at

For context, Jungle Scout's published Catalyst tiers on 20 August 2026 were $49, $79 and $149 a month on monthly billing, or $29, $49 and $129 a month billed annually, with extra seats at $49 a month or $459 a year each — so on a small team the seat line, not the plan line, is usually what pushes people to look elsewhere.

  • Helium 10 — the strongest reverse-ASIN and keyword mining in the category, plus listing tooling and coverage beyond Amazon. This is the natural move if competitor keyword depth is the item on your list. Its published entry paid tier sits above Jungle Scout's, so it is a step up in both capability and cost rather than a saving.
  • SmartScout — built around brand and seller mapping rather than product hunting. If your question is who owns what in this category and where the white space is, it answers a question the others mostly do not.
  • Keepa — price, rank and offer history in depth, for very little money. If your list has "historical data" on it and nothing else, this is a much cheaper answer than a full suite.
  • Data Dive — keyword and listing analysis for people who already know what they are doing and want depth rather than onboarding.
  • ZonGuru — a comparable all-rounder aimed at the same buyer as Catalyst, worth a trial if the issue is fit rather than capability.

Are you replacing a tool, or leaving the category?

There is a pattern in who searches this phrase, and it is worth naming because it changes the answer entirely.

Research suites are built for a phase — the phase where the central question is what should we sell, and how should we describe it. That phase is intense, it is where the money is made or lost, and a good suite is worth every penny during it.

Then the phase ends. The catalogue settles. The listings are written. Rank is established. And the questions that fill the week change completely: why did the fulfilment fee on that ASIN go up, how many days of cover do we have on the hero SKU, why is the return rate on the blue one double the black one, why has storage doubled since spring, and what happened to the units that shipment says we sent.

A brand asking those questions and shopping for a research tool is doing something subtly self-defeating: renewing against last year's bottleneck. The tool is not underperforming. It has finished. And no alternative in the same category — cheaper, denser or friendlier — will answer a single one of the questions in that paragraph, because none of them are research questions.

The test is simple. Look at your last twenty tool sessions. If most were about choosing or describing products, buy the best research suite for your list above. If most were about fees, stock, returns or money Amazon owes you, you have graduated, and the next purchase belongs in a different aisle.

Before you cancel: the export list

Whatever you switch to, do these four things while you still have a login. Every one of them is painful to reconstruct afterwards and takes minutes now.

  • Export your keyword banks and tracked-term history. Rank history is the asset you cannot rebuild — nobody backfills it for you, and a new tool starts its clock the day you connect it. Get it out as a file.
  • Export your product research shortlists and notes. The reasoning behind why you rejected forty candidates is genuinely valuable six months later, and it lives nowhere else.
  • Screenshot or export your alert configuration. Listing change alerts and their thresholds take real time to rebuild, and rebuilding from memory guarantees gaps.
  • Run one final competitor snapshot of the categories you care about, so you have a dated baseline to compare against once the new tool has warmed up.

Then set the cancellation date in a calendar rather than trusting yourself to remember, and start the replacement's trial before the old subscription lapses so you have a fortnight of overlap. A gap of even three weeks in rank tracking is a hole in your record you will still be annoyed about next year.

Which one you should actually pick

If you still need research, check Amazon's free first-party reports first, then buy the narrowest paid tool that covers what is genuinely missing — Helium 10 for competitor keywords, a history specialist for price and rank, a mapping tool for category structure. If your weekly questions have moved to fees, stock and recovery, you have not outgrown Jungle Scout so much as finished with the category.

What to do with this

Before you switch, write down the one number the switch has to change — reorder timing, storage fees, fee misclassification, or reimbursements recovered. Then ask the new vendor to show you how their product moves that specific number, and what happens on the weeks nobody logs in.

Common questions

What is the best free Jungle Scout alternative?

Amazon's own reporting, and it is not a consolation prize. With a Professional account and Brand Registry you get Search Query Performance, Top Search Terms, Product Opportunity Explorer, Market Basket and Repeat Purchase analysis, and the fee preview reports — first-party data drawn from actual orders rather than modelled from rank. It is slower to work with than a polished suite and it will not show you a competitor's keywords, but for demand sizing and your own term performance it is better data than anything you can buy.

Is Helium 10 a cheaper alternative to Jungle Scout?

No — on published rates it is a step up in price as well as in depth. The reason to move is capability, particularly reverse-ASIN keyword mining and listing tooling, not saving money. If cost is what sent you looking, the cheaper paths are Amazon's free first-party reports, a narrow specialist tool for the one job you actually need, or dropping to a lower tier where you are and accepting fewer seats.

Do I need a research tool at all once I am established?

Less than you think. Once your catalogue is settled, most of what a research suite offers duplicates data you already have, and the questions that dominate your week become operational rather than exploratory. Many established brands are best served by a cheap history tool, Amazon's own reports, and putting the rest of the budget where the margin is actually leaking. Review it at renewal instead of letting it roll for another year by default.

Does Dr. Stock replace Jungle Scout?

No. We do no product research, no keyword mining and no listing construction, and pretending otherwise would be a category error anyone using these tools would spot instantly. Dr. Stock runs Amazon inventory and supply chain economics — reorder timing, aged stock, fee errors, inbound discrepancies, recovery and returns cost. If you are in launch mode, buy a research suite. If your catalogue is settled and your margin is thinning, that is what we are for.

What should I export before cancelling a research subscription?

Keyword banks and rank tracking history first, because no new tool backfills it and the record simply stops. Then your research shortlists and the notes explaining why candidates were rejected, your alert configuration and thresholds, and one dated competitor snapshot per category you care about.
