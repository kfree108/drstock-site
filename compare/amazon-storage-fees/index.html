Amazon storage fees: what you are charged, why, and how to lower the bill

The short answer

Amazon charges FBA sellers a monthly storage fee based on the space your stock occupies, with a higher rate in the holiday peak. On top of that come surcharges for aged inventory, overfull storage and sometimes thin stock. Current rates are on Amazon's own fee page, and change by year.

The charges that make up your storage bill

Most pages treat "storage fees" as one line. On your statement it is several charges, each triggered by a different behaviour. Knowing which one you are paying is the first step to lowering it.

  • Monthly inventory storage fee. Charged on the space your units take up in Amazon's fulfilment centres, measured in cubic feet and averaged over the month. The rate depends on the size tier of the product and on the time of year, with the holiday peak costing more than the rest of the year.
  • Aged inventory surcharge. A further monthly charge on units that have sat in a fulfilment centre past the age threshold Amazon publishes. It stacks on top of ordinary storage.
  • Storage utilization surcharge. Applies when the amount of stock you hold is high relative to your recent sales, so it rewards stock that moves.
  • Low-inventory-level fee. Applies in the opposite situation: when your stock cover is thin relative to your sales for a sustained period.
  • Removal and disposal fees. Not storage as such, but they are the bill you pay when you choose to take stock out instead of paying to keep it there.

We have deliberately printed no rates here. Amazon revises them, and a number copied from a page that was accurate last year is how sellers end up with a wrong budget. Read the current rate card on Amazon's seller site and check the date on it.

How to estimate your own monthly bill

You do not need software to get a first estimate. You need three inputs from Seller Central and one rate from Amazon's fee page.

  • Step 1: volume per unit. Take the product's dimensions as Amazon has them recorded (not as you measure them) and calculate cubic feet per unit.
  • Step 2: average units in stock. Use the month's average, not today's count. A big inbound shipment that arrives late in the month costs less than one that sat all month.
  • Step 3: the rate. Look up the rate for your size tier and the month on Amazon's fee page.

Then: storage fee = cubic feet per unit × average units in stock × the monthly rate per cubic foot. Add any aged-inventory or utilization surcharge Amazon shows against the SKU, and you have the figure to compare with your statement.

If your estimate and the statement disagree, the most common cause is the dimensions. Storage and fulfilment fees are both billed against the measurements Amazon holds. If those are larger than the real product, you pay too much on every unit, every month, until the record is corrected.

Why the fee is bigger than the fee line suggests

The headline rate is rarely the expensive part. Three effects do most of the damage.

  • Slow stock compounds. A unit that sells in a month pays for a month. A unit that does not sell pays every month, then picks up the aged-inventory surcharge on top. Cost per unit rises faster than most sellers expect.
  • The peak is a planning problem. Stock sent in for the holiday season is charged at the higher peak rate for as long as it sits there, including any units left over in January. Over-ordering for peak is an expensive way to find out.
  • Storage is only half the cost of dead stock. The cash tied up in units that have stopped selling cannot be used to buy the products that are selling. That opportunity cost is not on any Amazon statement.

This is why the useful question is not "what is the rate?" but "which of my SKUs will be charged a surcharge next month, and what am I going to do about each one before the assessment date?"

A checklist to lower your storage fees

  • Run the inventory age report monthly and list every SKU approaching Amazon's aged-inventory threshold. Decide before the line is crossed, not after.
  • For each aged SKU, pick one action: run a promotion, create a removal order, liquidate, or accept the charge knowingly. A decision you made on purpose is better than a charge you discovered.
  • Check the dimensions on your top SKUs. If Amazon's record is larger than the real product, request a remeasure and keep the case number.
  • Plan peak shipments against realistic sell-through, not best-case demand. Leftover peak stock is billed at peak rates.
  • Watch your stock cover on fast sellers. The low-inventory fee and lost sales from stockouts both come from the same forecasting gap.
  • Audit your statement against your estimate each month, and look for fees on stock that was lost, damaged or never received. Some of those are eligible for reimbursement.

None of this requires a particular tool. A spreadsheet and a monthly calendar reminder handles it for a small catalogue. It becomes a job for software or a team when the number of SKUs grows past what one person can review each month.

Common questions

How much are Amazon storage fees?

It depends on the product's size tier, the month and how long the stock has been there. Amazon publishes the current rate card on its seller site, and it changes from year to year, so read the page that is live today rather than a number from a blog post. Then use the formula on this page to turn the rate into a monthly estimate for your catalogue.

When does Amazon charge storage fees?

Monthly, on the stock you held during the previous month. The fee is calculated from the average space your units occupied, so what matters is how long your stock sat there, not just what you held on one day.

How do I avoid Amazon aged inventory fees?

Check the inventory age report every month, and act on each SKU approaching Amazon's age threshold before it is crossed. The options are a promotion, a removal order, liquidation or accepting the charge on purpose. Sending in smaller shipments that match your sell-through also keeps fewer units on the shelf long enough to age.

Why are my Amazon storage fees higher than I expected?

The usual causes are dimensions that Amazon has recorded larger than the real product, stock left over after the holiday peak, and aged units that now carry a surcharge. Compare your statement with the estimate from this page's formula to see which one it is.

Can I get a refund on Amazon storage fees?

Not for fees that were correctly charged. You may be able to recover money for units Amazon lost or damaged, and for fees charged against wrong dimensions once the record is fixed. Reimbursement windows are limited, so file promptly.
