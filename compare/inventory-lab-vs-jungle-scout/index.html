The split, in one paragraph

Almost every page comparing these two builds a feature grid, which hides the only thing that matters: they sit on opposite sides of the purchase order.

Jungle Scout is a pre-buy tool. Product database, opportunity finder, keyword research, sales estimates, supplier database, rank tracking. Everything it does is aimed at the question "should I put money into this?" Once the answer is yes and the money is spent, most of the product goes quiet.

InventoryLab is a post-buy tool. Margin calculated before an item goes on the buy list, a guided five-stage shipment workflow that replaces Seller Central's process, MSKU generation and box content labels, unified inventory across FBA, merchant-fulfilled and unlisted stock, and a built-in profit and loss by product, supplier or category. Everything it does assumes the stock exists.

So "which is better" is the wrong question and "which do I need first" is the right one. If you are still deciding what to sell, the research tool. If you have inventory arriving and you are building shipments by hand in Seller Central, the operations tool. Buying the wrong one first is the most common mistake on this comparison, and it is expensive in time rather than money.

What each one actually costs, with the billing basis on every figure

A 15% reimbursement commission applies on recoveries and sits outside the subscription.

The naming matters when you are comparing quotes. inventorylab.com is live and trading and describes its own workflow, while the billing and the plan ladder belong to Threecolts — both things are true at once, and it is the same shape as several other renames in this market. The public records, with counts, and one we will not average

Every score below came from the platform that issues it, read 21 August 2026. A score without its count is not a fact.

  • Jungle Scout — G2 4.6 from 243 reviews, Capterra 4.7 from 285, Trustpilot a TrustScore of 4.1 from 3,940. Three independent platforms, a combined sample well over four thousand, and no sharp disagreement between them. That is a strong and coherent record, and the honest reading is that people who use it are largely satisfied.
  • InventoryLab — Trustpilot shows a TrustScore of 3.0 from 6 reviews against inventorylab.com. Six is not a rating, we decline to average it, and we would decline just as firmly if those six had been five stars.
  • InventoryLab has no standalone G2 listing. A search for the name returns Threecolts Seller 365, which carries 4.8 from 10 reviews — again a sample too small to conclude from, though it points at the larger Threecolts record: Trustpilot 4.4 from 67.

The asymmetry here is real and it is worth naming plainly rather than dressing up: one of these products has a large, checkable public record and the other does not. That is not evidence InventoryLab is worse. It is evidence that a tool absorbed into a bundle stops accumulating reviews under its own name, which is a fact about corporate structure rather than software quality — and it means the InventoryLab decision has to be made on a trial rather than on ratings.

Whichever you read, sort by date and take the last twelve months. Both corpora span several product generations.

Where each one genuinely wins

Jungle Scout wins when the decision ahead of you involves money you have not yet spent. Its historical depth is the specific thing to buy it for — one month of keyword history on the entry tier, a year on the middle tier and two years at the top. In most categories seasonality is the variable that decides whether a product is a good idea, and you cannot see a season in a month. It also wins on marketplace breadth for research, covering eight marketplaces on Catalyst.

InventoryLab wins when the bottleneck is your hands. The five-stage guided shipment process replacing Seller Central's flow, MSKU generation, box content labels, and profit calculated per product, supplier and category on the same underlying records as the shipments — that is time, not information. For a reseller or arbitrage operation processing hundreds of units a week, the hours saved dwarf the subscription. Nothing in Jungle Scout does this.

Which is why most operators running either one seriously end up wanting both, and why the honest comparison is sequential rather than competitive. You research, you buy, you ship, you sell, you research again — and the second lap is when the profit-and-loss data from the operations tool starts improving the research decisions.

The decision neither of them informs, and one that catches people out

Both tools are built around a single marketplace at a time, and the biggest decision many sellers face is not which product to buy next but whether to open another marketplace at all. Neither answers it, so here is the guidance we give our own clients.

Reviews carry to a new marketplace at roughly 90 to 95% when the listing uses the same product identifier — and account health is isolated per marketplace, so a violation in one does not follow you to another. Those are two standing pieces of guidance from our own book rather than promises, and together they are why the United Kingdom is the usual first international step for a US brand: the review base transfers, the language does not change, and a compliance problem there cannot damage the US account. Germany is the harder second step, where personal-care and packaging regulation adds real cost before the first sale.

Note what that changes about the tooling question. A research subscription that covers eight marketplaces is telling you about demand. It is not telling you about the review base you already own, which is frequently the larger asset — and it is the reason a second marketplace is often a better use of capital than a third product.

What both tools show you and neither one fixes

Here is the boundary, and it is the same for both. These are tools you drive. They surface numbers accurately and they act on nothing.

The Amazon-side lines that sit outside both: fulfilment fee bands set from Amazon's recorded package dimensions rather than the ones in your system, aged-inventory surcharges accruing per unit against a calendar, short claim windows on shipment discrepancies and damaged units, reorder timing that prevents a stockout rather than reporting it, and the removal-versus-liquidation decision on stock that stopped moving. A profit and loss will show you the charge. It will not tell you the charge was avoidable.

The honest test before you buy either subscription: name the decision you will make in the next ninety days with this data, and name who will make it. If you cannot name the person, the subscription is the wrong purchase regardless of which one you pick — and adding a second dashboard to a business where nobody has four to ten hours a week changes nothing.

Which one you should actually pick

Buy Jungle Scout while the money is still unspent — its historical keyword depth is the thing worth paying for, and its public record across three platforms is strong. Buy InventoryLab, now inside Threecolts Seller 365, when the bottleneck is hands rather than information. Most serious operators end up with both. What to do with this

Neither of these files a fee dispute for you. Before you pick, run one check: take your ten highest-volume ASINs, compare Amazon's recorded package dimensions against your own measured dimensions, and flag anything where the size tier looks wrong. Then ask each vendor what happens next — who measures, who files, who follows up.
