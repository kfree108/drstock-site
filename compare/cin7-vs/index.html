Finish the sentence first, because each ending has a different answer

This is a half-typed search. What follows it varies enormously, and the six endings lead to six unrelated decisions. Before shortlisting anything, find yourself in this list.

  • Cin7 vs an ERP. Deciding question: does your finance function need manufacturing, multi-entity or consolidated accounting inside the same system? If yes, you are shopping above this category, and the implementation is a project rather than a purchase.
  • Cin7 vs a peer platform. Deciding question: which one integrates cleanly with the specific accounting package, 3PL and marketplace mix you already run? Feature parity in this tier is closer than the marketing suggests; integration fit is where the differences are real.
  • Cin7 vs a 3PL. Deciding question: are you buying software or buying labour? These are different purchases and confusing them is the most expensive error on this page.
  • Cin7 vs an analytics tool. Deciding question: do you need a system of record, or do you need the truth about margin? Those cost an order of magnitude apart.
  • Cin7 vs a spreadsheet. Deciding question: how many people need to be right about stock at the same time?
  • Cin7 vs changing nothing. Deciding question: is your problem actually inventory, or is it Amazon fee and storage economics wearing an inventory costume?

Most buyers who arrive here believe they are in comparison two. In our experience the majority are in comparison five or six, and answering the wrong one costs a year and a five-figure implementation.

Cin7 vs Cin7 — settle this before anything else

There are two products under this name, and comparing the wrong one against your shortlist produces nonsense.

Read from Cin7's own pricing page on 20 August 2026, in US dollars and excluding tax, Cin7 Core lists three plans billed monthly: Standard at $349 a month, Pro at $599 and Advanced at $1,199. Each is banded by annual order volume — 6,000, 24,000 and 120,000 respectively. Cin7 Omni publishes nothing and is quoted per customer, with no preset volume limits.

Two traps live in that paragraph. The first is the annual band: 6,000 orders a year is 500 a month, which sounds generous until you count how orders are actually generated. Every sales channel contributes, business-to-business orders count, and a shipment split across two locations can consume more than one. Brands routinely scope themselves into the entry tier on marketplace volume alone and discover the real total is double.

The second is that a comparison built on Core's published figures tells you nothing about what Omni would cost. If your shortlist includes enterprise platforms, you are comparing against the quoted product, and you will need the quote before any of the arithmetic means anything.

One habit worth carrying into every vendor conversation, including with us: check which billing tab and which currency you are reading, and confirm whether a volume band is monthly or annual. Those three checks catch most of the bad numbers in circulation.

Cin7 vs a spreadsheet — the comparison most people are really in

Nobody writes this comparison up, because there is no affiliate commission on a spreadsheet. It is still the most common real decision, and it has an honest test.

You need a system of record when more than one person has to be right about stock at the same time, and not before. Three questions settle it:

  • How many people touch inventory numbers in a week? One person with a well-built sheet is faster than any platform. Three people editing the same sheet is a data corruption incident waiting for a busy Friday.
  • How many places can stock physically be? One fulfilment channel is manageable in a sheet. The moment stock sits in FBA, a 3PL and a garage simultaneously, reconciliation by hand stops being viable.
  • What breaks when the sheet is wrong for a week? If the answer is an awkward conversation, stay put. If it is overselling, cancelled orders and a metrics problem on your seller account, buy the system.

If all three answers point at the spreadsheet, keep it and spend the money on stock. A platform bought before those thresholds delivers an implementation project, a training burden and a monthly bill in exchange for solving a problem you do not yet have. That is not a criticism of the software — it is a criticism of buying it early, which is a mistake nobody selling it has an incentive to warn you about.

Cin7 vs a 3PL — software and labour are not substitutes

This confusion causes more wasted money than any feature gap, and it comes from both directions.

A system of record tells you what you have and where. It reconciles channels, holds costs and pushes stock levels around. It does not employ anybody, does not lease a building and will not pick a carton. Meanwhile a fulfilment provider stores and ships goods, and its own systems are built for running its floor rather than for being your commercial source of truth.

Brands that buy the software expecting fulfilment relief find the picking still needs doing. Brands that outsource to a provider expecting a system of record find they now have less visibility than before, because the authoritative numbers live in somebody else's platform under somebody else's definitions.

A third option sits alongside both. Flexible or on-demand warehousing — Flexe is the best-known name — sells space and handling on shorter commitments than a lease, and publishes no rate card, quoting per requirement. That solves a capacity problem rather than a systems problem. Warehouse platforms such as Extensiv also quote on a call rather than publishing figures, which is normal for configured deployments and worth knowing before you build a comparison spreadsheet expecting numbers to fill it.

The clean way to think about it: software answers where is my stock and what did it cost. A provider answers who is going to move it. Nearly every brand of scale needs both, and neither substitutes for the other at any price.

Cin7 vs an analytics tool — an order of magnitude apart, on purpose

If what you actually want is to know your true margin by SKU, a system of record is an expensive way to find out.

Sellerboard, read the same day, runs from $19 to $79 a month billed monthly, or $179 to $759 for a year, banded by monthly order count and seats. Against $349 a month for the entry tier of a multichannel platform, that is not a close comparison on price — because they are not doing the same job.

An analytics product reconciles money: fees, returns, promotions, advertising and your cost of goods, producing a defensible profit figure after the fact. A system of record governs operations: it is the single authority multiple channels and people read from, and it is what stops you overselling. One tells you the truth about the past. The other keeps the present consistent.

The sequencing advice that falls out of this is worth more than either product. Buy the truth first. Reconciliation is cheap, fast to implement and frequently reveals that the crisis prompting a platform search was a margin problem rather than a systems problem. If, having seen accurate numbers, you still have three people arguing about stock across four channels, then buy the platform — with better information about which tier you actually need.

Cin7 vs changing nothing — the Amazon economics no platform touches

Here is the sixth comparison, and the reason this page exists on a site about Amazon inventory rather than about ERPs.

A great many platform searches begin because Amazon margin is falling and inventory feels like the culprit. Often it is not. These four lines are unaffected by any system of record you install:

  • Size-tier and dimension errors. Fulfilment is billed against the measurements Amazon holds. When they are wrong, you overpay on every unit shipped until a remeasure is requested and pushed to a conclusion. Your platform stores the correct dimensions and has no route to making Amazon adopt them.
  • The 181-day threshold. Amazon's FBA page states that units held in a fulfilment centre beyond 181 days attract an aged-inventory surcharge, charged monthly on top of ordinary storage. A platform will show you stock age. Deciding between removal, liquidation, a price move and absorbing the charge is a human call against a fixed date.
  • Claim windows. Inbound shipments that reconcile short, units lost or damaged, removals that never arrived — each has a filing window counted in days. A discrepancy your system correctly flagged earns nothing once the window closes.
  • Capital in dead stock. The largest number on many brands' balance sheets is inventory that has stopped selling, paying rent while it sits. No platform will make the call to release it.

Before committing to a platform migration, price those four. If they are larger than the operational chaos you were trying to fix — and for Amazon-heavy brands they usually are — you have been comparing the wrong things.

Which one you should actually pick

Buy Cin7 Core when several channels and several people must agree about stock and your combined annual order volume is real. Buy an ERP when finance and manufacturing must share a system. Buy analytics first if you simply want margin truth. Keep the spreadsheet if one person can hold it. And price the Amazon-side leaks before assuming a platform will close them.

What to do with this

Neither of these files a fee dispute for you. Before you pick, run one check: take your ten highest-volume ASINs, compare Amazon's recorded package dimensions against your own measured dimensions, and flag anything where the size tier looks wrong. Then ask each vendor what happens next — who measures, who files, who follows up.

Common questions

What is Cin7 usually compared against?

Six things, and they are not interchangeable: full ERPs, peer multichannel inventory platforms, third-party logistics providers, profit analytics tools, spreadsheets, and doing nothing at all. Each comparison turns on a single question that is rarely about features — finance scope, integration fit, software versus labour, system of record versus margin truth, how many people must be right at once, and whether the underlying problem is really inventory. Identify which one you are in before you build a shortlist.

What is the difference between Cin7 Core and Cin7 Omni?

Core is the published product — three plans read at $349, $599 and $1,199 a month on 20 August 2026, in US dollars excluding tax, banded by annual order volume at 6,000, 24,000 and 120,000. Omni is the enterprise product, configured per customer, with no preset volume limits and no published figures. Comparing a shortlist against Core's prices tells you nothing about what Omni would cost, so establish which one your requirements point at before doing any arithmetic.

Do I need Cin7 if I only sell on Amazon?

Often not, and it is worth being honest about that. A system of record earns its cost when several channels and several people must agree about stock simultaneously. A single-channel brand with one fulfilment path and one person managing stock is usually better served by a profit analytics tool and Amazon's own inventory reports. If your Amazon margin is the actual worry, a platform will govern your data beautifully and change none of the fee, storage or claim economics that are draining it.

Is Cin7 a replacement for a 3PL?

No. Software tells you what you have, where it is and what it cost; a fulfilment provider employs the people who move it. Neither substitutes for the other at any price. Brands that buy the platform expecting fulfilment relief still have picking to do, and brands that outsource expecting a source of truth often end up with less visibility, because the authoritative numbers now sit in someone else's system under someone else's definitions. Most brands of scale need both.
